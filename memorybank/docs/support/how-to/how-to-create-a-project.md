# How to Create a Project

A **project** is one funded grant or programme with one donor and one reporting cycle. Only **Admins** can create projects. Check that you have a free project slot on your plan ([Plans and limits](/support/account-billing/plans-and-limits)); archived projects do not count.

You create a project with the **project wizard**: ten short slides, one decision at a time. **Nothing is created until you press Start the project on the last slide**, so you can fill the slides in any order, leave, and come back. Your answers are kept as a draft.

## Before you start

You do not need documents. You can answer every slide yourself. If you have a proposal, logframe, work plan or budget, the wizard can read it and propose answers for you to confirm (see "Start from documents" below).

It helps to have to hand: the donor's name, the project dates, your results (goal, outcomes, outputs, activities) and indicators, the places you work, and the budget.

## Step 1: Open the wizard

1. Click **New project** on **Projects**, or open **+ Record** in the top bar and choose **New project** under **Create**, or press **Ctrl K** and type *create a project*. The wizard is the only way to create a project (the old one-page form was retired in Version 4.000).
2. The wizard opens on slide 1. Across the top are ten numbered tabs. Each shows how many questions are **still to answer**. A tab is red and says "n to answer" while something is missing on that slide, and shows **✓ Complete** when the slide is finished. Click any tab to jump to it, or use **Next →** and **← Back**.
3. The bar **Still to answer (n)** lists every missing answer. Click one to go to it.
4. Under the tabs, tick **Only what is still needed** if you want **Next →** to skip slides that are already complete (for example after the starting answers filled most of them) and take you straight to what is missing, then to the last slide.

Read the yellow notice at the top once. The wizard reads documents with an AI that can miss information or misread a figure, so **check each item against your documents. You are responsible for what you accept.**

## Step 2: Go through the ten slides

### 1. Start
Say what kind of project it is.
- **Donor** and **Country**: type them.
- **Main sector** (a list of about 30 humanitarian and development sectors): this decides the starting answers and the indicator library you are offered. You can add other sectors too.
- **Donor wording for the results framework**: choose your donor's terms for the levels (USAID, FCDO, European Union or United Nations), or leave DonorDesk's own words. The Results framework slide uses them at once, and the project keeps them when it starts. You can change them later in the project's **Settings → Setup → Reporting profile**.
- Optional: **Choose files** (up to five documents) and **Read my documents** (see "Start from documents" below).
- **Use the starting answers for this sector** fills in the usual reporting frequency, review mode, entry window, common risks and a starting dashboard. It only fills what is still empty, and you can change every answer afterwards. In a test it completed the **Data entry** and **Reporting** slides at once. Your organisation can write its own starting answers: see [How to write your organisation's starting answers](/support/how-to/write-your-own-starting-answers).

### 2. Project and team
- **Project name**, **Project code** (must be unique in your workspace), **Start date**, **End date**, **Currency** (for example USD) and a **Short summary**.
- **Team**: type a **Name** and **Email**, choose a **Role** (**Project manager**, **Monitoring and evaluation officer**, **Grants officer**, **Field officer**, **Compliance officer** or **Viewer**) and an **Invitation** (**Invite later** or **Invite when the project starts**), then click **Add to team**. The slide requires **a project manager and a monitoring and evaluation officer**. People without an account are invited when you launch (if you chose so) and join the project when they accept.
- **Outside partner (sees this project only)**: add anyone from another organisation, or tick **There are no outside partners**. See [How to invite an outside partner](/support/how-to/invite-an-outside-partner).

### 3. Results framework
What the project will achieve, from goal down to activities.
- **Choose from the library** opens the sector library. Click **Add the suggested results** to add your sector's goal, outcomes, outputs and activities in one go (about 20 items). You can also pick groups in the library.
- **Add a result** adds one by hand: choose its **Kind** (**Goal**, **Outcome**, **Output**, **Activity**, or your donor's words for them), write what will be achieved, and choose what it **Belongs to**.
- Each outcome and output has **Logic (optional)**: **Why this leads to the result above it** and **Assumptions**. The library's suggested results arrive with a starting assumption you can keep, change or delete. A note reminds you of outcomes and outputs without their reasoning; it is a suggestion only and never stops the launch.
- Where your documents or the library gave no codes, the wizard makes them up and asks you to check them: click **Keep the N codes made up for you** to accept.
- Edit or **Remove** any result. Every outcome needs an output and every output needs an activity.

### 4. Indicators
How you will know each result is reached.
- **Choose from the library**: filter by result level, kind, **Recommended only** or **With a reference standard**; click **Select the recommended ones** (or **Select this group**, or tick single indicators), choose where they go under **Put them under** (**The result at each indicator's own level** is the default), then click **Add selected: N**. Or use **Add an indicator** (type **What will be measured**, choose what it **Measures**, click **Add an indicator**).
- Each indicator then gets a card with: **Measures**, **Kind of number**, **Unit**, **Starting value** (or tick **No starting value**), **Target**, **How often**, **Break down by** (separate with commas), **Responsible** (someone from your team) and **Better when** (**Higher** or **Lower**).
- Every indicator needs its kind, frequency, starting value and target, an owner and a direction before the slide is complete. **Fill in for all indicators with nothing yet** sets **How often** and **Break down by** for all cards at once.
- **Use suggested settings for all (n)** appears when some cards still have a gap. It fills only what is empty: the **Kind of number** (a rate or percentage when the unit or name says so, a number when the target is a plain number), **Better when** (lower for deaths, defaulting, non-response and stock-outs, higher for work done and people reached; a name it cannot judge gets nothing) and **Responsible** (the monitoring and evaluation officer, only when exactly one is on the team). It never changes a choice you already made, and you can change any card afterwards.
- If you read an indicator **sheet**, a **Type** column (Number, Percentage, Ratio) and a **Break down by** column are read too, so those cards arrive filled in.
- Every result needs at least one indicator; the slide names any that has none.
- Library indicators carry the label **From the sector library**, may show a **Reference** (for example *at most 20 people per toilet*) that is **not your target**, and start as **not yet reviewed**. Check each one before relying on it.

### 5. Places
Where the project works.
- Choose the **Country**, then tick the areas the project works in from your places list (use **Search areas**). Ticking an area includes everything inside it.
- **Other places** are places that are not areas on the map, such as a camp, a clinic or a village: type a **Name**, choose what it is **Inside**, and click **Add a place**.
- Or tick **Not used for this project**.

### 6. Work plan
When and where each activity happens.
- **Add an activity**: type the **Activity** and choose which logframe activity it is **Part of**, then click **Add an activity**. The slide counts *Activities with planned work: n of N*; **every activity in your logframe needs planned work**.
- **Links we suggest** lists indicators the activity probably feeds, each with a reason. Click **Accept all** (or tick some and **Accept n selected**), or **Not this one**. Nothing is linked until you accept.
- Each planned activity needs **Starts**, **Ends**, **Led by** (a team member), a place (**Project-wide** or the areas you ticked) and the indicators it **Feeds**. A note appears if some work **feeds no indicator**.
- Each indicator must be fed by at least one activity, or it has no way of being collected.

### 7. Data entry
How the numbers will be collected, checked and proven.
- **How each indicator is collected**: choose **A form in the field**, **Another tool (KoboToolbox or SurveyCTO)**, **Typed in** or **Worked out from other indicators**. The wizard suggests one, with its reason; click **Use this** or **Not this one**.
- **How values are checked**: **Simple** (values are handed in as they are saved, and a person who can verify checks them when a file is attached) or **Full** (every value is handed in, then a reviewer verifies it). Who verifies follows each person's role.
- **When values can be entered**: **Opens (days before the period ends)** and **Closes (days after the period ends)**. Reviewers can always enter a late value.

### 8. Reporting
- **How often the project reports**: monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, annual, final or custom.
- **Each kind of report**: **Add a kind of report** with its layout and due date (days after the period ends).
- **The reporting calendar** shows the reports that will be created.
- **Remind people before reports are due**: on or off.

### 9. Budget and finance
- **Funding**: type **From** (the funder), **Amount** and **Currency** (three letters, for example USD), then **Add funding**. Amounts must be written plainly (`1250.50`, not `1,250`).
- **Budget**: **Add a budget line** with a name, amount and currency. **Does it add up?** compares funding with the budget and never adds different currencies together.
- **Reporting currency**: click the suggested currency (for example **Use USD, the currency of the funding**) or choose another.
- Or tick **Not used for this project** if you keep finance elsewhere. Who approves spending follows each person's role.

### 10. Review and launch
- **Risks and assumptions**: risks suggested by your starting answers are marked **Suggested**; click **Confirm** on each, or **Confirm all N that are waiting**, or **Remove**. Add your own with **Add a risk or assumption** (**What it is**, kind, **How serious**). The slide also needs your assumptions: add them, or tick **Not used for this project**.
- **Checklist**: nothing to set up here. The compliance checklist is built from your data and the donor's requirements as each report is prepared.
- **Starting dashboard**: choose one (there is one per sector), or tick **Not used for this project**.
- **Invitations**: who will be invited.
- **What will be created**: the complete list, for example *20 results · 12 indicators · 2 places · 12 planned activities · 1 funding source*.
- **Sign off**: available once every slide is complete. Any later change clears the sign-off, so sign off last.
- **Start the project**.

## Step 3: Start the project

Press **Start the project**. The wizard creates the project and everything you set up in order and shows each step (**Project details**, **Results**, **Indicators**, **Reporting periods**, **Places**, **Work plan** and so on) as **Done**. Then it says **The project has been started** with an **Open the project** link. If something stops it, press **Start the project** again: it resumes and never creates anything twice.

The new project is **Active** at once. Its first reporting period is created (for example a quarterly report for the first three months), and its **Settings → Setup** page shows **READY**. The later periods and the closing report are created as you go: see [How to create a reporting period](/support/how-to/create-a-reporting-period).

People you added without an account do not appear on the project's **Settings → Team** until they join; after that they are assigned automatically. The person who launched the project is not assigned by default: use **Settings → Team → Assign member** on the project to add yourself or others.

## After launch: the project's Settings → Setup page

Open the project's **Settings → Setup** page. It is headed **Prepare your project for reporting** and lists what reporting needs, each marked **Done** or **Todo** with a **Set up** button:

1. **Project workspace folder** – the folder tree in Google Drive.
2. **Donor template** – usually the one item still to do after a wizard launch: [upload and approve one](/support/how-to/upload-donor-template). Without one, reports use the built-in structure.
3. **Logframe and indicators** – for example *23 reportable of 23*. When some are not reportable yet it says why, for example *44 reportable of 55. 11 need a starting value.* A quantitative indicator needs a starting value, a target, a unit and a frequency before it counts as reportable.
4. **Reporting profile** – language and tone.
5. **Team assignment** (recommended).

**Blockers** lists anything that stops reporting. **Worth reviewing** lists things that do not stop you but change what the report can say, for example *15 indicators need their calculation confirmed*, with a **Review** button ([how an indicator is read](/support/getting-started/indicators-and-targets)). When the status says **READY**, click **Mark setup complete**, then [create the reporting periods](/support/how-to/create-a-reporting-period). **Retry workspace** and **Repair workspace** fix the Drive folders if they failed.

Things you do **not** set in the wizard: who verifies, who approves reports and finance, the evidence rules and the compliance checklist. Verification and approval follow people's roles, confidentiality is chosen per file, and the checklist is built from the donor template. Set the report template under **Reports → Donor templates** ([upload a donor template](/support/how-to/upload-donor-template)).

## Start from documents (optional)

On slide 1 click **Choose files** (a proposal, logframe, work plan or budget, up to five), then **Read my documents**. Spreadsheets (CSV or Excel) of the logframe, indicators and work plan are read fully and quickly; a Word proposal is read less completely. If **Read my documents** says "The request timed out", choose fewer files (three sheets read well together) and enter the places and the budget on their own slides.
1. Files are kept in your organisation's Google Drive, so Drive must be connected ([Connect Google Drive](/support/how-to/connect-google-drive)). The AI features must be turned on for your organisation.
2. Each slide then shows what was found, with **Show in the document** beside each item. Accept, edit or reject each one. Nothing is created until you launch.
3. A section of your document that is blank stays blank. The wizard says so rather than inventing content. Items it could not place are listed as **left out**.
4. **Read this part again** re-reads one part of a document.

## Edit or end a project later

Open the project's **Settings → Project settings** to edit details, dates and budget and to set the status. See [How to archive a project](/support/how-to/archive-a-project).

## Try it safely first

The Academy **demo project** is a complete sample project that you can explore, and it does not count toward your limits. See [Academy tour](/support/how-to/use-the-academy-tour).
