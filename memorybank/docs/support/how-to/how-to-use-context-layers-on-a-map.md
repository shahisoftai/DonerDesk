# Use context layers on a map

Context layers help people find their way on a map: where people live, where health facilities are, where the towns and settlements are. They are context only. Nothing is reported against them, and they never change your results.

## Choose which layers your maps offer (administrators)

1. Open **Where we work → Manage places** and go to **Map context layers**.
2. Tick the layers your organisation wants:
   - **Where people live (population)** (WorldPop)
   - **Health facilities** (Healthsites.io and OpenStreetMap)
   - **Settlements and towns** (Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team)
3. Choose **Save map settings**.

## Switch a layer on

On a project's map, the **Context** list beside the map shows the layers your organisation offers. They are off until you tick one, and they stay as you set them only while the page is open.

## Your own imagery (optional, administrators)

You can add satellite or aerial imagery from a provider you choose, by giving its tile address (it starts with `https://` and contains `{z}`, `{x}` and `{y}`) and the credit the provider asks for.

**Read this first:** imagery is the one map feature that is loaded from outside DonorDesk. The provider sees which area of the map is being looked at, though never your project's data. Leave the address empty to keep every map on DonorDesk's own servers. Imagery is never shown through a share link or to outside partners.

## Good to know

- Every layer's source and licence is listed on the [map sources page](/maps/sources).
- The health facility and settlement layers are under the Open Database Licence. A copy of the data we use can be had on request.
