# UNHCR Reporting Requirements

UNHCR is one of the largest humanitarian donors and one of the most structured. Its reporting requirements are detailed,严格要求 evidence quality, and cover specific themes that UNHCR prioritises across all programmes.

## Overview of UNHCR Reporting Framework

UNHCR operates primarily through partner agreements governed by:

- **Project Partnership Agreement (PPA)** — The legal framework governing the partnership
- **Operational Plan (OPLAN)** — Annual plan negotiated between UNHCR and the partner
- **UNHCR Monitoring Framework** — Risk-based approach to oversight

Reporting obligations typically include:

- **Quarterly progress reports** (narrative + output data)
- **Annual reports** (comprehensive, including outcome-level assessment)
- **Situation reports (Sitreps)** — For emergency or fast-changing contexts
- **Financial reports** — Quarterly or on a disbursement schedule

## Key UNHCR Reporting Themes

Every UNHCR partner report must explicitly address:

### 1. Protection

UNHCR's protection mandate means protection reporting is not optional — it is the foundation.

**Include:**
- Number and type of protection incidents documented
- Proportion of beneficiaries with protection concerns addressed
- Specific protection interventions (legal aid, GBV response, child protection)
- Accountability to affected populations (AAP) mechanisms in place

**Key indicators UNHCR tracks:**
- # of people reached with protection monitoring
- # of GBV cases referred to services
- # of children in alternative care arrangements
- % of complaints received that were addressed within 30 days

### 2. Age, Gender, and Diversity (AGD)

UNHCR applies an AGD approach. Reports must disaggregate by:

- Sex (male/female — always mandatory)
- Age (0–4, 5–11, 12–17, 18–59, 60+)
- Diversity factors relevant to the context (disability, ethnicity, statelessness status)

**Tip:** UNHCR uses the "IASC Age, Gender and Diversity Marker" — familiarise your team with it.

### 3. Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNHCR requires evidence that:

- Communities participate in programme design and monitoring
- Feedback mechanisms exist and are accessible to all groups, including marginalised ones
- Community feedback is acted upon and changes are communicated back

**Include in your report:**
- Description of community engagement approaches
- Number and nature of feedback received
- Changes made to programming as a result of feedback

### 4. Settlement Approach and Localisation

UNHCR increasingly prioritises working through and with local and national partners.

**Include:**
- How local actors are involved in implementation
- Capacity building activities with national/local partners
- Progress towards localisation commitments (Grand Bargain)

## UNHCR Output Categories

UNHCR uses a standardised output framework. Familiarise yourself with the UNHCR Results Framework — it has specific output codes and indicators that should be used in your reports.

When reporting against UNHCR, always reference:

- The specific **output code** from the OPLAN
- The **indicator** as stated in the agreed logical framework
- The **baseline** and **target** from the approved proposal

Example output reference:
> "Output 3.1 — Health: Improved access to primary health care for refugees and host community. Indicator 3.1.1: # of primary health care consultations provided. Achieved: 18,420 consultations against a Q2 target of 15,000."

## UNHCR Reporting Format

### Quarterly Report Structure (Recommended)

1. **Executive Summary** (1 page)
2. **Operational Context** — Changes in the displacement situation, operating environment
3. **Progress Against OPLAN** — By output area, with indicators, baselines, targets, actuals
4. **Cross-Cutting Themes** — Protection, AGD, Environment, AAP
5. **Challenges, Risks, and Mitigation Measures**
6. **Coordination and Partnerships**
7. **Visibility and Communication**
8. **Financial Summary**
9. **Annexes** (data sheets, monitoring reports, assessment reports)

## UNHCR Financial Reporting Requirements

UNHCR financial reports must:

- Follow the UNHCR budget format (typically in the grant amendment or PPA annex)
- Show expenditure by budget line vs. approved budget
- Include a variance explanation for any line exceeding 10% variance
- Be certified by your organisation's finance manager or auditor

> **Common UNHCR audit finding:** Expenditure reported in the narrative does not match the financial report. Always cross-check before submission.

## UNHCR Monitoring and Evaluation

UNHCR conducts partner monitoring through:

- **Field monitoring visits** — UNHCR staff visit programme sites
- **Remote monitoring** — For security-restricted environments
- **Partner self-monitoring** — Using UNHCR's tools ( PRIM (Partnership Results and Impact Monitoring) or equivalent)
- **Joint assessments** — With clusters, government, or other partners

Your reports should reference monitoring activities conducted:

> "A joint UNHCR-partner monitoring visit was conducted on 14 May 2026 to Settlement Zone C. 18 beneficiaries were interviewed (10 female, 8 male). Key finding: water point functionality was 94%, exceeding the 85% standard. Minor issue identified: latrine cleaning supplies were insufficient in Block 7. Partner responded by distributing supplies within 5 days."

## Common UNHCR Reporting Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Missing disaggregation** — UNHCR will return a report that does not disaggregate by sex
2. **Incorrect output codes** — Always verify you are using the current OPLAN output codes
3. **No reference to protection** — Every report should address protection, even if there were no incidents
4. **Late submission** — UNHCR has strict deadlines; late reports may affect future funding
5. **Reporting on activities instead of outcomes** — UNHCR wants to see what changed, not just what you did
