# EU Grants Reporting

The European Union funds humanitarian aid through DG ECHO (civil protection and humanitarian aid) and development cooperation through INTPA (International Partnerships, formerly DEVCO). The EU has a complex regulatory framework, with detailed rules for both financial and narrative reporting. Understanding which EU directorate general (DG) funds your project is the first step to understanding your reporting obligations.

## EU Funding Instruments

### For Humanitarian Aid

- **DG ECHO** — See [DG ECHO Reporting](/support/report-writing-skills/donor-specific/dg-echo) guide
- Funding under the **Humanitarian Aid Regulation (Council Regulation 1257/96)**

### For Development Cooperation

- **INTPA** — International Partnerships (formerly DEVCO)
- **NDICI-Global Europe** — Neighbourhood, Development and International Cooperation Instrument
  - Geographic programmes (country and regional)
  - Thematic programmes (human rights, civil society, food security, etc.)
  - Emergency, crisis, and response programmes
- **IPA** — Instrument for Pre-Accession Assistance (candidate and potential candidate countries)
- **ENI** — European Neighbourhood Instrument (now part of NDICI)

### For Civil Protection

- **UCPM** — Union Civil Protection Mechanism (DG ECHO handles this)

## EU Grant Agreement Structure

Every EU grant is governed by:

1. **The Grant Agreement** — The legal contract
2. **Annexes** — The detailed conditions:
   - **Annex I** — Description of the action (action plan, methodology, deliverables)
   - **Annex II** — General conditions (standard template)
   - **Annex III** — Budget (detailed cost breakdown)
   - **Annex IV** — Procurement rules
   - **Annex V** — Reporting requirements and templates
   - **Annex VI** — Communication and visibility requirements

**Read Annex V first.** It tells you exactly what to submit and when.

## EU Narrative Reporting Requirements

### Periodic Reports

Most EU grants require **periodic reports** aligned with payment tranches:

- **Narrative report** — Technical description of activities, outputs, and outcomes
- **Financial statement** — Expenditure by budget category
- **Summary for publication** — A short public-facing summary (for grants above €500,000)

### Final Report

Required within **6 months** of the project end date:

- Full narrative covering the entire project period
- Final financial statement
- Summary of results against the original proposal
- Data on the **12 EU mandatory indicators** (for development projects)

### Report Structure for INTPA Projects (NDICI)

1. **Action Summary** (stand-alone, 1–2 pages — published on EU website)
2. **Technical Report**
   - Context and needs analysis update
   - Progress against the action plan (by work package / output)
   - Achievement of outputs vs. targets
   - Achievement of outcomes (using OECD-DAC criteria)
   - Partnership and coordination
   - Cross-cutting issues (gender, environment, human rights, climate)
   - Risks and mitigation measures
   - Lessons learned
   - Annex: Indicator tracking table
3. **Financial Report**
   - Detailed expenditure by budget line
   - Certificate on financial statements (if above €750,000)

## The 12 EU Mandatory Indicators (Development Projects)

For NDICI geographic programmes, the EU has **12 mandatory outcome-level indicators** that must be reported:

1. Number of people reached by food security interventions
2. Number of people with improved access to water and sanitation
3. Number of children (0–5) with access to quality early childhood development services
4. Number of people with enhanced physical access to critical infrastructure
5. Number of people with improved access to clean energy
6. Number of people with improved access to digital services
7. Number of people reached by health interventions
8. Number of people covered by climate change adaptation measures
9. Number of people covered by biodiversity conservation measures
10. Number of people with enhanced skills (education and vocational training)
11. Number of women and girls with improved access to sexual and reproductive health services
12. Number of civil society organisations and movements strengthened

**If your project contributes to any of these indicators, you must report against them specifically.**

## EU Financial Reporting Requirements

### Eligible Costs

The EU has strict **eligibility rules**. Costs must be:

- **Incurred during the eligibility period** (as specified in the grant agreement)
- **Foreseen in the budget** (as approved in Annex III)
- **Necessary** for the implementation of the action
- **Reasonable and justified** — backed by evidence

### Cost Categories

| Category | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Personnel costs** | Staff directly working on the action | Salaries, social charges, pensions |
| **B. Travel and subsistence** | Transport, accommodation, per diems | Flight tickets, hotel, daily allowances |
| **C. Equipment and supplies** | Assets purchased for the action | Vehicles, computers, generators |
| **D. Other direct costs** | Subcontracting, publications, audit, bank charges | External audits, translation, visibility |
| **E. Indirect costs** | Overhead (capped at 7% of direct eligible costs) | Office rent, utilities, management time |

### Pre-financing and Payment Tranches

EU grants typically operate on a **pre-financing system**:

1. EU pays an initial pre-financing amount (e.g., 30% of total grant)
2. You spend the money and report expenditure
3. When pre-financing is >70% spent, you can request the next tranche
4. Each tranche is paid based on approved periodic report

### Financial Controls

- **Double funding** is strictly prohibited — do not claim the same cost from two sources
- **In-kind contributions** (volunteer time, third-party donations) must be valued at fair market value
- **Exchange rate losses** are your organisation's risk — budget contingency for currency fluctuations
- **Auditor's certificate** — Required for final financial statements for grants above €750,000

## EU Communication and Visibility Requirements

The EU requires all grant recipients to **acknowledge EU funding** prominently:

### Mandatory Visibility Elements

- EU flag and funding statement on all publications, websites, and communications
- Statement: *"This [document/programme/action] is funded by the European Union"* (or equivalent)
- EU emblem on all equipment purchased with EU funds
- Donor visibility in all press releases and social media

### What Is Not Allowed

- Using the EU emblem in a way that implies EU endorsement of your organisation (not just the project)
- Statements suggesting the EU is responsible for your organisation's broader work
- Use of EU logo on items unrelated to the funded action

## EU Rules on Subcontracting and Tenders

Your grant agreement (Annex IV) specifies **procurement rules**. For most EU grants:

- Contracts above **€10,000** require competitive tendering
- Contracts above **€60,000** require documented competitive process with at least 3 quotes
- Contracts above **€140,000** (for works) or **€215,000** (for supplies/services) require formal tender procedure

Your narrative report should describe any significant subcontracting and confirm compliance with procurement rules.

## EU Gender and Human Rights Requirements

### Gender Equality

- All NDICI-funded projects must contribute to gender equality
- Gender marker must be reported (similar to OECD-DAC)
- Specific **gender equality indicators** must be included in your logframe

### Human Rights

EU development policy is guided by a **Human Rights-based Approach (HRBA)**:

- All projects must be screened for human rights risks
- Projects affecting indigenous peoples require **Free, Prior and Informed Consent (FPIC)**
- Human rights defenders — any incidents affecting human rights defenders should be reported immediately

### Environment and Climate

- **Strategic Environmental Assessment (SEA)** screening required for all projects
- Climate proofing required for infrastructure projects
- Biodiversity impact assessment required for projects in sensitive ecosystems

## Common EU Reporting Mistakes

1. **Missing mandatory indicators** — EU will return reports that omit the 12 mandatory indicators
2. **Financial-narrative mismatch** — Every budget line variance must be explained in the narrative
3. **Late submission** — EU has strict deadlines; late reports can lead to suspension of payments
4. **Missing visibility** — EU emblem and statement missing from publications
5. ** ineligible costs claimed** — Know the eligibility rules before submitting financial statements
6. **Inconsistent conversion rates** — Use the rate specified in your grant agreement for currency conversion
7. **Under-reporting partnership** — EU expects explicit description of the partnership arrangement
