# FCDO and Bilateral Donors Reporting

The UK's Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office (FCDO) is one of the largest bilateral donors globally, alongside USAID, GAC (Canada), BMZ (Germany), and Sida (Sweden). While each bilateral donor has specific requirements, they share common frameworks rooted in the OECD-DAC evaluation criteria and results-based management approaches.

## FCDO's Operating Model

FCDO operates through a variety of funding mechanisms:

- **Logical Framework (Logframe)** — The primary management tool for most FCDO programmes
- **Theory of Change (ToC)** — Required for all FCDO-funded programmes above £5 million
- **Business Case** — Required before funding is approved; defines the intervention logic
- **Operation Framework** — For humanitarian and rapid-onset programmes

FCDO places strong emphasis on **Value for Money (VfM)**, **gender equality**, **disability inclusion**, and **results that can be measured independently**.

## The FCDO Logical Framework

FCDO uses a standardised **Logical Framework (Logframe)** with four levels:

1. **Impact / Goal** — The ultimate intended change (usually at outcome level)
2. **Outcome / Purpose** — The change the project is expected to contribute to during implementation
3. **Outputs** — The specific, tangible products or services delivered
4. **Activities** — The actions undertaken to produce the outputs

### FCDO Output-Outcome Distinction

FCDO is strict about the output/outcome distinction:

**Output** = What you produced or delivered (e.g., "1,200 mosquito nets distributed")
**Outcome** = The change that resulted from that output (e.g., "child malaria incidence in target villages decreased by 34%")

Your reports must demonstrate the **causal link** between outputs and outcomes, not just list activities.

## FCDO Reporting Requirements

### Annual Review / Annual Report

Most FCDO programmes require an **Annual Review** — a structured assessment of performance against the Logframe, submitted by the implementing organisation to FCDO.

The Annual Review is scored using the **FCDO Annual Review scoring framework**:

| Score | Description |
|---|---|
| A+ | Excellent — exceeds expectations |
| A | Very Good — meets expectations with minor shortcomings |
| B | Satisfactory — meets expectations with some shortcomings |
| C | Poor — falls short of expectations |
| D | Very Poor — falls significantly short |

### Annual Review Structure

1. **Executive Summary** (2–3 pages, standalone)
2. **Progress Against Logframe** — Output by output, outcome by outcome
3. **VfM Analysis** — Cost-effectiveness, economy, efficiency, equity
4. **Cross-Cutting Issues** — Gender, disability, climate, safeguarding
5. **Risk and Issue Log** — Current status of risks
6. **Partnership and Coordination**
7. **Lessons Learned**
8. **Financial Summary** — Budget vs. actual, variance explanation
9. **Next Period Priorities**

## FCDO Cross-Cutting Themes

### Value for Money (VfM)

FCDO requires VfM analysis in all reports. The FCDO VfM framework has four dimensions:

1. **Economy** — Minimising the cost of resources used (inputs)
2. **Efficiency** — Minimising waste in converting inputs to outputs
3. **Effectiveness** — Achieving the desired outcomes
4. **Equity** — Ensuring fair distribution of benefits, particularly to marginalised groups

For each, describe:
- What was planned
- What was achieved
- How it compares to alternatives or benchmarks

### Gender Equality

FCDO's **Gender Equality Act** (2014, updated 2023) requires all international development programmes to:

- Have a **Gender Equality Marker** score (0, 1, or 2 — with 2 being gender equality as a primary objective)
- Demonstrate how gender equality was integrated into programme design
- Report sex-disaggregated data for all relevant indicators
- Include a **Women's Economic Empowerment** narrative where relevant

### Disability Inclusion

FCDO's **disability policy** requires:

- **Disability disaggregated data** — Track beneficiaries with disabilities separately
- **Inclusive programme design** — Accessibility of programme activities and communications
- **Mainstreaming** — How disability is integrated across all programme components, not siloed

### Climate and Environment

FCDO requires all programmes to:

- Screen for **climate risks** at design stage
- Report on **climate adaptation** and **mitigation** measures where relevant
- Describe **environmental safeguards** compliance

## FCDO Safeguarding

FCDO has strict **safeguarding requirements** for all partners:

- **Prevention of Sexual Exploitation, Abuse and Harassment (PSEAH)** — Mandatory policies, training, and reporting
- **Child safeguarding** — Specific obligations for programmes working with children
- **Whistleblowing** — Mechanisms for staff and beneficiaries to report concerns

Your reports must include:
- Number of safeguarding concerns reported and status of each
- Actions taken in response to concerns
- Any SEA allegations — must be reported immediately (not at reporting period)

## Other Major Bilateral Donors: Key Differences

### USAID (United States)
- **Federal regulations** govern many aspects of implementation
- Detailed **ADS (Automated Directive System)** compliance required
- **Branding and marking** requirements are very specific
- **Cost allowability** rules are strict (FAR / CFR compliance)
- See full USAID guide in [USAID Reporting](/support/report-writing-skills/donor-specific/usaid-reporting)

### GAC (Global Affairs Canada)
- Similar logframe approach to FCDO
- **Policy for Women's Economic Empowerment** — explicit requirements
- **Indigenous peoples** — specific obligations for projects affecting Indigenous communities
- Strong emphasis on **gender equality** and **inclusive development**

### BMZ (Germany / GIZ/KfW)
- **Budget support** mechanisms alongside project funding
- Strong **sustainability** and **institutional capacity building** focus
- **Evaluation** is a formal requirement, often with external evaluators
- Requires reporting in **German** for some funding streams

### Sida (Sweden)
- Strong emphasis on **rights-based approach** and **gender equality**
- Uses **OECD-DAC evaluation criteria** as the evaluation framework
- Requires **theory of change** and explicit assumption documentation
- **Civil society** and **democracy** support are priority sectors

## Common FCDO and Bilateral Reporting Mistakes

1. **Output vs. outcome confusion** — FCDO is strict; do not describe outputs as outcomes
2. **Missing VfM narrative** — FCDO expects explicit VfM analysis, not just financial data
3. **Safeguarding not reported** — Must be addressed even if there were no incidents (confirm "zero")
4. **Gender marker incorrect** — Ensure the gender marker score matches the programme design
5. **Theory of change assumptions not revisited** — FCDO expects teams to review assumptions annually
6. **Results chain is incomplete** — Activities → Outputs → Outcomes → Impact must all be connected

## Bilateral Reporting: Key Universal Principles

Regardless of which bilateral donor you work with:

1. **Read your grant agreement** — It specifies exactly what is required and when
2. **Know your logframe** — Every paragraph in your narrative should map to a logframe row
3. **Disaggregate everything by sex** — At minimum; add disability, age, location as relevant
4. **Evidence is mandatory** — Every result claim needs a documented evidence source
5. **Challenging context is expected** — Include it honestly; donors fund organisations that manage reality well
