# DG ECHO Reporting Requirements

The Directorate-General for European Civil Protection and Humanitarian Aid Operations (DG ECHO) is one of the world's largest humanitarian donors, funding both international organisations and NGOs through Humanitarian Aid operations and Civil Protection mechanisms.

## DG ECHO's Approach to Reporting

DG ECHO has a reputation for rigorous, evidence-based reporting. It operates on a **results-based management (RBM)** approach — meaning it expects to see clear chains from activities to outputs to outcomes.

**Key DG ECHO principles:**

- ** Humanity** — Humanitarian imperative first
- **Impartiality** — Aid based on need, not other criteria
- **Independence** — Operational autonomy from political agendas
- **Neutrality** — No taking sides in armed conflict
- **Accountability** — To both donors and affected populations

## Types of DG ECHO Funding and Their Reporting

### HIP-Funded Operations (Humanitarian Implementation Plans)

Most DG ECHO funding comes through annual Humanitarian Implementation Plans (HIPs), which set out funding priorities by crisis.

**Reporting requirements:**
- **Six-monthly operational reports** (mandatory)
- **Final report** (within 6 months of project end)
- **SitReps** for fast-onset emergencies
- **Financial reports** aligned to the DG ECHO cost classification system

### Crisis Modifier

If your DG ECHO-funded project includes a **crisis modifier**, it requires:

- A separate trigger matrix (pre-agreed thresholds)
- Separate reporting on when and how the modifier was activated
- A budget amendment process

### Civil Protection (UCPM)

Funding through the Union Civil Protection Mechanism requires additional reporting on:

- National civil protection coordination
- European Civil Protection Pool contributions
- Lessons learned from disaster response operations

## The DG ECHO Logical Framework (Logical Framework Approach)

DG ECHO requires a **specific logical framework (logframe)** for each grant. The logframe has:

- **Specific Objective** — The overall goal (usually at outcome level)
- **Specific Objectives** — Intermediate outcomes (usually 2–3 per project)
- **Expected Results** — Outputs (usually 4–8 per specific objective)
- **Actions/Activities** — The work that produces the outputs

**Critical rule:** Your report must follow this logframe structure exactly. Do not reorganise or rename objectives in your report — use the exact wording from the approved logframe.

## DG ECHO Reporting Format

### Six-Monthly Operational Report Structure

1. **Executive Summary** (max 1 page)
2. **Context and Crisis Analysis Update**
3. **Implementation Progress by Specific Objective**
   - Progress per expected result
   - Indicator performance table
   - Challenges and mitigation measures
4. **Cross-Cutting Issues** (protection, gender, environment, accountability)
5. **Partnership and Coordination**
6. **Visibility and Communication**
7. **Lessons Learned**
8. **Indicators Performance Table** (annexed)
9. **Financial Report** (annexed)

## DG ECHO-Specific Themes

### 1. Humanitarian Principles

DG ECHO places strong emphasis on humanitarian principles. Your report should:

- Describe how humanitarian principles guided programme decisions
- Note any instances where principles were challenged and how they were upheld
- Reference Sphere Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) alignment where relevant

### 2. Protection

DG ECHO is a strong advocate for protection mainstreaming. Every report should address:

- How protection risks were identified and mitigated
- Specific protection activities (GBV prevention, child protection, legal aid)
- Proportion of total activities budget dedicated to protection (% target: usually 15–20%)

### 3. Gender Equality

DG ECHO requires explicit gender equality reporting:

- Gender analysis conducted and integrated
- Gender markers used (DG ECHO uses a specific 0–2 gender marker)
- Disaggregation by sex (mandatory)
- Specific actions to address gender-based inequalities

### 4. Environment and Climate

DG ECHO increasingly requires climate and environmental reporting:

- Environmental impact assessment conducted (for infrastructure projects)
- Climate adaptation measures integrated
- Do No Harm analysis completed

## DG ECHO Cost Classification

DG ECHO uses a specific cost classification system:

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| **A — Direct Costs** | Directly attributable to the action (staff, activities, equipment) |
| **B — Indirect Costs** | Overhead, administration, support functions |
| **C — Special Costs** | Pre-agreed exceptional costs (audit, evaluation, visibility) |

Only **Category A costs** can be claimed as match funding or counted towards co-financing.

**Important:** DG ECHO does not fund 100% of costs. Most NGOs receive 50–90% of total costs. The remaining amount must come from other sources and be documented in your financial report.

## DG ECHO Financial Requirements

- Use the **DG ECHO budget format** — not your organisation's internal format
- All costs must be traceable to supporting documentation
- Staff costs must be supported by timesheets or contracts
- In-kind contributions must be valued at market rates and documented
- Any budget revision exceeding **15%** of any budget line requires prior DG ECHO approval

## DG ECHO Indicators and Measurement

DG ECHO expects:

- **Baseline** for every indicator (from proposal)
- **Target** (from proposal)
- **Actual** (from your monitoring data)
- **% achievement** (actual vs. target)

For qualitative indicators, DG ECHO expects a **narrative description** of change, not just a yes/no.

## DG ECHO Monitoring

DG ECHO conducts:

- **Field monitoring visits** — Usually through its network of field offices (ERCU in East Africa, etc.)
- **Remote monitoring** — For high-risk or hard-to-reach areas
- **Annual monitoring plans** — Shared with partners at the start of the year

Your report should reference monitoring visits and note how findings were acted upon.

## DG ECHO Language and Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| HIP | Humanitarian Implementation Plan — the annual strategic document |
| FPA | Framework Partnership Agreement — for multi-year funding |
| ERCU | ECHO Regional Civil Protection and Humanitarian Aid — DG ECHO's field office in East Africa |
| RBM | Results-Based Management |
| CHS | Core Humanitarian Standard |
| Gender Marker | DG ECHO's 0–2 scale measuring gender equality integration |

## Common DG ECHO Reporting Mistakes

1. **Changing logframe wording** — Always use the exact approved logframe terminology
2. **Missing baseline data** — Always restate the baseline in each report
3. **Financial-narrative inconsistency** — Cross-check every figure in both documents
4. **Late submission** — DG ECHO has strict deadlines; late reports require a formal explanation
5. **Generic protection language** — DG ECHO expects specific, evidence-based protection reporting
6. **Missing gender marker** — Always include the gender marker score
