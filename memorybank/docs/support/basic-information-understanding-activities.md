# Understanding Activities

Activities are the field updates that document what your team did during a reporting period: a training, a distribution, a monitoring visit, a meeting.

## Activity vs logframe activity

The **logframe** describes planned activities as part of the results framework. **Activities** in the Activities tab are the *records of what actually happened*, with dates, places, participants and narrative.

## What you record

- **Activity title**, **date**, **reporting period** and **location**
- **Summary**
- **Participants**: total, female, male, children, and participants with disability
- **Achievements**, **challenges**, **lessons learned** and **next steps**
- Linked **evidence** (files that prove it happened)

## Statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Draft | Saved, not submitted. |
| Submitted | Waiting for review. |
| Needs revision | Sent back with a note. |
| Accepted | Confirmed. |
| Rejected | Not accepted (note required). |

## How activities feed reports

The AI Reporter uses submitted activities for the period: narratives, participant numbers, challenges and lessons learned support the matching sections, and linked evidence is cited as a source. Your activity text is also what **Tell the Story** builds on.

## Compliance

The checklist flags late activity updates and activities without supporting evidence.

## Tips

1. Log regularly, not just at report time.
2. Be specific: "Trained 20 CHWs on ANC screening", not "Training".
3. Include numbers and places.
4. Attach evidence.
5. Be honest about challenges; donors value learning.
