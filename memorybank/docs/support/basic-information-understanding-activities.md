# Understanding Activity Tracking in DonorDesk

Activities are the day-to-day tasks your team carries out. Tracking activities helps document what was done and provides evidence for donor reports.

## What Is an Activity?

An activity is a specific event or task completed as part of your project. Examples:
- "Conducted community health awareness session on 3 August"
- "Distributed nutritional supplements to 50 households in Camp A"
- "Held quarterly review meeting with partner organisations"

## Activity vs Output

Activities are the **actions** you take. Outputs are the **results** of those actions.

| | Activity | Output |
|--|----------|--------|
| What it is | A task or event | A product or deliverable |
| Example | "Conducted training" | "20 health workers trained" |
| Tense | Present/future | Past (completed) |
| In logframe | Lowest level | Above activity |

## When to Log Activities

Log an activity when:
- An event happens (training session, meeting, distribution)
- A milestone is reached
- A significant task is completed
- Something notable occurs that should be documented

## What to Record for Each Activity

### Basic Information
- **Title** — Short name for the activity
- **Date** — When it happened
- **Location** — Where it took place
- **Description** — What exactly happened

### Participation
- **Total participants** — How many people
- **Male / Female / Children** — Breakdown by group
- **People with disabilities** — If tracked

### Quality
- **Achievements** — What was accomplished
- **Challenges** — What went wrong or was difficult
- **Lessons learned** — What you would do differently
- **Next steps** — Follow-up actions needed

### Links
- **Output** — Which output this activity relates to
- **Indicators** — Which indicators this activity affects
- **Evidence** — Files that prove the activity happened

## Activity Statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Draft** | Saved but not submitted |
| **Submitted** | Sent for review |
| **Accepted** | Reviewed and confirmed |
| **Needs Revision** | Reviewer found issues |
| **Rejected** | Cannot be used as evidence |

## How Activities Feed Into Reports

When you generate an AI report draft:
1. Activity narratives are included in the Activities Completed section
2. Participation numbers go into the Beneficiary Reach section
3. Challenges become the Challenges section
4. Lessons learned become the Lessons Learned section
5. Evidence linked to activities supports compliance

## Activity and the Compliance Checklist

The compliance checklist checks:
- Which activities have evidence attached
- Whether that evidence is verified
- Whether activities are linked to outputs and indicators

Good activity tracking = good compliance scores.

## Tips for Good Activity Logging

1. **Log regularly** — Do not wait until the end of the period
2. **Be specific** — "Trained 20 CHWs on ANC screening" not just "Training"
3. **Include numbers** — Number of participants, sessions, materials distributed
4. **Link everything** — Connect activities to outputs, indicators, and evidence
5. **Be honest about challenges** — Donors appreciate learning from challenges
6. **Note lessons learned** — Future projects benefit from this insight
