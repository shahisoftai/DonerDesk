# Advanced: Data Privacy and GDPR Compliance

If your organisation operates in the EU or handles EU residents' data, you need to comply with GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation).

## What Is GDPR?

GDPR is a European Union law that protects personal data. It gives individuals rights over their data and requires organisations to handle it responsibly.

## How DonorDesk Supports GDPR

### Data Processing Agreement

DonorDesk provides a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) for organisations that need one. Contact **support@donordesk.online** to request a DPA.

### Data Retention

Your data is retained:
- As long as your account is active
- For a period after account deletion per our retention policy
- In compliance with applicable laws

### Security Measures

DonorDesk implements:
- Encryption in transit and at rest
- Access controls and authentication
- Audit logging
- Regular security assessments

## Your Responsibilities as a User

### Collecting Personal Data

When you use DonorDesk, you may collect personal data about:
- Beneficiaries (mentioned in evidence, activities)
- Staff members (in user accounts)
- Third parties (in documents)

You are responsible for:
- Having legal basis to collect this data
- Getting necessary consents
- Handling data securely
- Responding to data subject requests

### Donor Reporting

When submitting reports to donors:
- Remove or redact personal data where not required
- Follow donor-specific data handling requirements
- Obtain consent for photos or identifiable information

## Data Subject Rights Under GDPR

### Right to Access

Individuals can request a copy of their data.

### Right to Rectification

Individuals can request correction of inaccurate data.

### Right to Erasure

Individuals can request deletion of their data.

### Right to Portability

Individuals can request their data in a machine-readable format.

## Handling Data Subject Requests

If you receive a data subject request:

1. Do not ignore it — GDPR requires response within 30 days
2. Verify the requester's identity
3. Assess whether the data is within DonorDesk or your systems
4. Contact DonorDesk support if data is in DonorDesk
5. Document the request and your response

## Beneficiary Data in Evidence

Evidence files often contain beneficiary data:

### What to Avoid

- Uploading files with beneficiary names and ID numbers unless necessary
- Including addresses, phone numbers, or other identifying details
- Sharing identifiable photos without consent

### What to Do

- Use attendance sheets that can be anonymised or aggregated
- Remove or redact unnecessary personal details
- Store highly sensitive evidence with restricted access
- Get informed consent before photographing identifiable individuals

## Consent Management

### When Consent Is Needed

- Photos of identifiable individuals
- Case studies featuring personal stories
- Any data sharing with third parties

### What Consent Should Cover

- What data is collected
- How it will be used
- Who it will be shared with
- How long it will be retained

## Donor-Specific Requirements

Some donors have specific data protection requirements:
- EU donors typically require GDPR compliance
- UN agencies have their own data protection policies
- Government funders may have national data protection laws

Always review donor data protection requirements before reporting.

If your organisation is required to have a Data Protection Officer (DPO), ensure they are aware of DonorDesk usage and can respond to related queries.
