[DEMO-7] Northern Ghana Maternal, Newborn & Child Health Resilience Programme (MNCH-R) — monthly report

Approved
1 Summary of the Month

July 2026 delivered the planned trainings and equipment for the European Union-funded Action, while flooding slowed outreach in Savelugu and Nanton. The project trained 30 health workers (21 female, 9 male) in EmONC, IMNCI and essential newborn care at Savelugu Municipal Hospital, for a total of 220 of 240, and 20 community health volunteers (12 female, 8 male) at Voggu CHPS compound, for a total of 300 of 300. Fifteen DHMT staff (11 female, 4 male) completed DHIMS2 and scorecard data-use training, reaching 60 of 60, and 10 facility managers (6 female, 4 male) completed respectful maternity care training, reaching 40 of 40. The project equipped 3 facilities with delivery kits and solar vaccine fridges, for 19 of 24 to date. Service delivery reached 26 integrated outreach clinics against a target of 360, down from 31, and 1,400 women attended community education sessions against a target of 18,000, down from 1,650. NHIS drives assisted 520 households against a target of 6,000, down from 560, for a total of 4,760, and district teams conducted 10 supportive supervision visits against a target of 120. Verified DHIMS2/GHS records show ANC4+ at 61% (target 70), skilled birth attendance at 69% (target 75), Penta3 at 82% (target 88), postnatal checks within 48 hours at 52% (target 60), no tracer-medicine stock-out at 78% of facilities (target 85) and complete, timely DHIMS2 reporting at 92% (target 95). Flooding and the lean season cut road access in Savelugu and Nanton for several days and two CHPS compounds closed temporarily; outreach was re-planned on higher ground routes and CHVs delivered ANC reminders on foot, which kept ANC reminders going in flooded communities. Evidence comprises the verified attendance sheet, handover document, outreach register, DHIMS2 extract, NHIS register and supervision report. Priorities: hold the next training cohort with mentoring visits, install the remaining kits and fridges, and review outreach routes against road conditions.

The Action reported progress by Outcome and Output of the Logical Framework Matrix for July 2026. Complete and timely DHIMS2 reporting was the strongest outcome measure at 92 percent against a 95 percent target, and no facility stock-out of tracer MNCH medicines stood at 78 percent against 85 percent. ANC4+ reached 61 percent against a 70 percent target, up 9 percentage points from the baseline of 52; skilled delivery 69 against 75; Penta3 82 against 88; and postnatal checks within 48 hours 52 against 60. 3 Activities Implemented

The Action carried out nine activities in July 2026, all dated within the reporting period, across Savelugu, Kumbungu and Nanton districts. The officer's narrative records that flooding and the lean season cut road access in Savelugu and Nanton for several days and two CHPS compounds closed temporarily; trainings nonetheless went ahead, and outreach in flood-affected communities was re-planned on higher ground routes with CHVs delivering ANC reminders on foot.

On 9 July 2026 at Savelugu Municipal Hospital, Savelugu District, the project trained 30 health workers in EmONC, IMNCI and essential newborn care with on-site mentoring (21 female, 9 male), bringing the life-of-Action total to 220 of 240. Verified evidence comprises the training attendance sheet and session records. The training records note that mentoring at the facility within a month of the classroom course improves use of the partograph and neonatal resuscitation.

On 10 July 2026 at the Savelugu District Health Directorate, Savelugu District, the project trained 15 DHMT staff in DHIMS2 and scorecard data use (11 female, 4 male); the life-of-Action total is 60 of 60. Verified evidence comprises the attendance and data-use session records. The records note that training on the district's own data is more useful than generic examples.

On 11 July 2026 at Voggu CHPS compound, Kumbungu District, the project trained 20 community health volunteers (12 female, 8 male); the life-of-Action total is 300 of 300. Verified evidence comprises the CHV training and session records. The records note that training CHVs at the nearest CHPS compound reduces absenteeism.

On 12 July 2026 at the Kumbungu District Health Directorate, the project trained 10 facility managers in respectful maternity care (6 female, 4 male); the life-of-Action total is 40 of 40. Verified evidence comprises the training attendance and session records. The records note that managers who hear women's exit-interview feedback commit to concrete changes.

On 14 July 2026 at Voggu CHPS compound, Kumbungu District, the project delivered and installed delivery kits (delivery sets, neonatal resuscitation equipment, fetal dopplers) and solar vaccine fridges at 3 facilities, bringing the life-of-Action total to 19 of 24. Handover certificates were signed by the in-charges and logged by the district health directorate. Verified evidence is the signed handover approval document. The records note that technician handover and a daily temperature log at installation keep fridges working after the project.

On 17 July 2026 in Kumbungu District communities, CHVs and community mobilisers held education sessions on antenatal care, facility delivery and newborn danger signs, reaching 1,400 women, all recorded as female; the life-of-Action total is 13,850 of 18,000. Verified evidence is the community session register. The records note that sessions at market days and water points reach more women than sessions at fixed venues.

On 19 July 2026 in Nanton District, NHIA district officers and CHVs ran NHIS enrolment and renewal drives, registering or renewing 520 households against a target of 6,000 and prioritising households with pregnant women and children under five; the life-of-Action total is 4,760 of 6,000. Verified evidence is the attached drive register. The records note that drives at antenatal clinics reach pregnant women directly.

On 20 July 2026 in the Nanton Health Centre catchment, Nanton District, the project held 26 integrated outreach clinics offering ANC, immunisation, postnatal checks and child weighing, against a target of 360; the life-of-Action total is 276 of 360. The DHIMS2 extract shows 61 percent of pregnant women with four or more ANC visits, 69 percent skilled deliveries, 82 percent Penta3 coverage and 52 percent postnatal checks within 48 hours at supported facilities. Verified evidence comprises the outreach register and the DHIMS2 extract. The records note that fixed outreach calendars shared with CHVs and chiefs raise turnout. The officer's narrative records that outreach and ANC slowed in Savelugu and Nanton because of the floods.

On 24 July 2026 in Nanton District, district teams and project staff made 10 supportive supervision visits using the standard checklist covering partograph use, stock management, cold chain and DHIMS2 completeness, against a target of 120; the life-of-Action total is 91 of 120. Verified evidence is the attached supervision report. The records note that a checklist with agreed actions that is reviewed at the next visit closes gaps faster than reports alone.

The activity records do not identify any planned activity that was not carried out in July; the trainings, equipment installation, education sessions, NHIS drives, outreach clinics and supervision visits listed above were all delivered. No quarterly scorecard review meeting is recorded in this month's activity records, and the indicator MR-OP2.3 (quarterly district scorecards) was not measured this period.

5 Challenges, Risks and Mitigation

The principal challenge of July 2026 is the disruption recorded in Activities Implemented: flooding and the lean season cut road access in Savelugu and Nanton for several days, and two CHPS compounds closed temporarily. The challenge is documented in the verified evidence attached to the month's activities: the outreach clinic register and DHIMS2 extract for the outreach clinics in Nanton, the supervision monitoring report, the training attendance sheet for the EmONC, IMNCI and essential newborn care course, the equipment handover document and the NHIS register.

The disruption slowed outreach and antenatal care in Savelugu and Nanton. The Action held 26 outreach clinics against a target of 360, reached 1,400 women against a target of 18,000, and assisted 520 households with NHIS enrolment or renewal against a target of 6,000. The trainings went ahead despite the disruption.

The project applied two mitigations. It re-planned outreach in the flood-affected communities on higher ground routes. Community health volunteers delivered ANC reminders on foot in flooded Savelugu communities. The lesson recorded by the team is that these foot visits sustained ANC reminders where roads failed.

The inputs record no other challenge or risk for July 2026. No request for support or a change that needs the Contracting Authority's agreement was recorded for the month.

6 Cross-Cutting Issues: Gender, Environment and Do-No-Harm

Flooding and the lean season cut road access in Savelugu and Nanton for several days in July 2026 and two CHPS compounds closed temporarily, shaping how the Action applied its cross-cutting commitments this month.

Gender. The reporting officer's statement records that community health volunteers reached pregnant women in flood-affected communities with referral advice. Sex-disaggregated results for the month show 30 health workers trained in EmONC, IMNCI and essential newborn care, comprising 21 female and 9 male; 20 community health volunteers trained and active, comprising 12 female and 8 male; 15 DHMT staff trained in data use, comprising 11 female and 4 male; and 10 facility managers trained in respectful maternity care, comprising 6 female and 4 male. Community education sessions reached 1400 women. The equipment installation, outreach clinics, NHIS enrolment drives and supportive supervision visits did not record sex-disaggregated participant data in the records supplied for this section.

Environment. The officer's statement records that flooded pit latrines at two CHPS compounds were reported to the district for disinfection. No further environmental monitoring record was supplied for July 2026.

Do-no-harm. The statement records that outreach teams avoided unsafe flooded routes and followed district safety guidance. No incident or safeguarding concern was recorded in the records supplied for the month. No complaints, feedback or safeguarding compliance documentation beyond this statement was supplied for the period.

8 Plan for the Next Month

For August 2026, the Action will carry forward the plans recorded in July's activity records, after the project delivered the month's trainings and equipment despite flooding in Savelugu and Nanton. The project will hold the next cohort of EmONC, IMNCI and essential newborn care training and follow up trained staff with mentoring visits, building on the 30 health workers trained in July (21 female, 9 male; 220 of 240 to date), since the training records note that mentoring at the facility within a month of the classroom course improves use of the partograph and neonatal resuscitation. The project will continue CHV cohorts and refresher sessions, following the 20 community health volunteers trained and active in July (12 female, 8 male; 300 of 300 to date), with training held at the nearest CHPS compound to reduce absenteeism. The remaining delivery kits and solar vaccine fridges will be installed as stock arrives, after 3 facilities were equipped in July (19 of 24 to date), with technicians continuing the handover practice of a daily temperature log. Community education sessions on antenatal care, facility delivery and newborn danger signs will continue, with radio spots added; sessions reached 1400 women in July (13,850 of 18,000 to date), and the records note that sessions at market days and water points reach more women than sessions at fixed venues. The project will hold the planned integrated outreach clinics and review outreach routes against the road conditions, after 26 clinics in July (276 of 360 to date). District teams will follow up the actions agreed at each facility during supportive supervision, using the checklist reviewed at the next visit; 10 visits were made in July (91 of 120 to date). DHMTs will be supported to use the new data views in monthly review meetings, following the data-use training for 15 DHMT staff in July (11 female, 4 male; 60 of 60 to date). The project will support the 10 facility managers trained in respectful maternity care in July (6 female, 4 male; 40 of 40 to date) to put their agreed action plans in place. NHIS enrolment and renewal drives will continue, with follow-up on renewals due next month, after 520 households were assisted in July (4,760 of 6,000 to date), and the drive records note that drives at antenatal clinics reach pregnant women directly. No numerical targets or expected results for August 2026 were recorded in the inputs, so none are stated here.
