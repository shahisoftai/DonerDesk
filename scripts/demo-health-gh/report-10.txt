[DEMO-7] Northern Ghana Maternal, Newborn & Child Health Resilience Programme (MNCH-R) — monthly report

Approved
1 Summary of the Month

August 2026 delivered 24 integrated outreach clinics against a monthly target of 360 (300 of 360 to date), across hard-to-reach communities, while flooding continued in the first half of the month. The project equipped 2 facilities (Zangbalun CHPS compound in Karaga District and one other) with delivery kits and solar vaccine fridges, bringing the total to 21 of 24; the last equipment shipment was held at customs, so deliveries were re-sequenced to the accessible districts first and clearance was followed up weekly with the clearing agent. Community education reached 1,300 women (down from 1,400; 15150 of 18,000 to date), and NHIS drives in Tolon and Karaga districts assisted 480 households (down from 520; 5240 of 6,000 to date). District teams conducted 9 supportive supervision visits (down from 10; 100 of 120 to date), covering partograph use, stock management, cold chain and DHIMS2 completeness. Verified DHIMS2/GHS records show ANC4+ at 60% (target 70), skilled birth attendance at 68% (target 75), Penta3 at 81% (target 88), postnatal checks within 48 hours at 52% (target 60), no tracer-medicine stock-out at 77% of facilities (target 85) and complete and timely DHIMS2 reporting at 91% (target 95). Priorities: install the remaining kits and fridges as stock arrives, hold planned clinics and follow up supervision actions. Evidence comprises the verified outreach register, DHIMS2 extract, handover document, attendance sheet, NHIS register and supervision report.

3 Activities Implemented

The Action carried out five recorded activities in August 2026, all dated within the reporting period, across Karaga and Tolon districts, while flooding continued in the first half of the month.

On 14 August 2026 at Zangbalun CHPS compound, Karaga District, the project delivered and installed delivery kits (delivery sets, neonatal resuscitation equipment and fetal dopplers) and solar vaccine fridges at 2 facilities, bringing the life-of-Action total to 21 of 24. Handover certificates were signed by the in-charges and logged by the district health directorate. Verified evidence is the signed handover approval document. The records note that technician handover and a daily temperature log at installation keep fridges working after the project.

In Karaga District communities on 17 August 2026, CHVs and community mobilisers held education sessions on antenatal care, facility delivery and newborn danger signs, reaching 1,300 women against a target of 18,000; the life-of-Action total is 15,150 of 18,000. All 1,300 participants recorded were female. Verified evidence is the attendance sheet. The records note that sessions at market days and water points reach more women than fixed venues.

In Tolon and Karaga districts on 19 August 2026, NHIA district officers and CHVs ran NHIS enrolment and renewal drives, registering or renewing 480 households against a target of 6,000 and prioritising households with pregnant women and children under five; the life-of-Action total is 5,240 of 6,000. Verified evidence is the attached drive register. The records note that drives at antenatal clinics reach pregnant women directly.

In the Tolon Health Centre catchment, Tolon District, the project held 24 integrated outreach clinics on 20 August 2026, offering ANC, immunisation, postnatal checks and child weighing, against a target of 360; the life-of-Action total is 300 of 360. The DHIMS2 extract shows 60 percent of pregnant women with four or more ANC visits, 68 percent skilled deliveries, 81 percent Penta3 coverage and 52 percent postnatal checks within 48 hours at supported facilities. Verified evidence comprises the outreach register and the DHIMS2 extract. The records note that fixed outreach calendars shared with CHVs and chiefs raise turnout.

In Tolon District on 24 August 2026, district teams and project staff made 9 supportive supervision visits using the standard checklist covering partograph use, stock management, cold chain and DHIMS2 completeness, against a target of 120; the life-of-Action total is 100 of 120. The supervision report records 91 percent DHIMS2 completeness and timeliness and no tracer-medicine stock-out at 77 percent of facilities. Verified evidence is the attached supervision report. The records note that a checklist with agreed actions reviewed at the next visit closes gaps faster than reports alone.

Which activities were planned but not carried out, and why? No activities were recorded as planned but not carried out in their entirety. The officer's narrative records that the last equipment shipment was held at customs, delaying the final delivery kits so that 21 of 24 facilities are equipped to date; the team re-sequenced deliveries to the accessible districts first and followed up the shipment clearance weekly with the clearing agent, and the shipment was released in September.

5 Challenges, Risks and Mitigation

Two challenges materialised in August 2026: flooding and a customs delay. Flooding continued in the first half of the month across the five districts, and an equipment shipment was held at customs, delaying the last delivery kits. The verified evidence records both challenges: the equipment handover document, the attendance sheet, the outreach clinic field visit register and DHIMS2 extract, the supervision monitoring report, and the NHIS register.

The customs delay left 21 of 24 facilities equipped to date. As described in the Summary of the Month, the project re-sequenced deliveries to the accessible districts first and followed up the shipment clearance weekly with the clearing agent; that weekly follow-up released the last equipment shipment in September. The Action nonetheless held 24 outreach clinics (down from 26 in the previous period) and reached 1,300 women through community education (down from 1,400) while the flooding continued.

The inputs record no other challenge or risk for August 2026. No request for support or a change that needs the Contracting Authority's agreement was recorded for the month.

6 Cross-Cutting Issues: Gender, Environment and Do-No-Harm

The cross-cutting challenge in August 2026 remained the flooding in the first half of the month and a customs delay on the last equipment shipment, both recorded in the evidence for this period. The reporting officer's statement records the situation on each theme.

Gender. The officer's statement records that women's exit-interview satisfaction continued to improve. On sex-disaggregated results, community education sessions reached 1300 women in August 2026, all female as recorded (0 male, 1300 female) per the verified attendance sheet. The equipment installation, outreach clinics, NHIS enrolment drives and supportive supervision visits did not record sex-disaggregated participant data in the records supplied for this section.

Environment. The statement records that flood damage to waste pits at two CHPS compounds was repaired. No further environmental or health-care waste monitoring record was supplied for August 2026.

Do-no-harm. The statement records that no incident was reported and that the customs delay did not affect patient safety. No safeguarding concern appears in the records supplied for the month. No complaints, feedback or safeguarding compliance documentation beyond this statement was supplied for the period.

Mitigation. Deliveries were re-sequenced to the accessible districts first and the shipment clearance was followed up weekly with the clearing agent; the statement records weekly follow-up finally released the last equipment shipment in September.
