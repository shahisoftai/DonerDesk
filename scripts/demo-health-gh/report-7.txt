[DEMO-7] NORTHERN GHANA MATERNAL, NEWBORN & CHILD HEALTH RESILIENCE PROGRAMME (MNCH-R) · MAY 1, 2026 – MAY 31, 2026 · DUE JUN 5, 2026

[DEMO-7] Northern Ghana Maternal, Newborn & Child Health Resilience Programme (MNCH-R) — monthly report

Approved
1 Summary of the Month

May 2026 delivered less than planned for the European Union-funded Action because an industrial action by nurses and community health officers closed or reduced services at many facilities in the five districts for about two weeks. The project held 18 integrated outreach clinics against a target of 360 (5% of target), down from 33 in the previous period, for a life-of-Action total of 219. Community education sessions reached 900 women against a target of 18,000, down from 1,700, for a total of 10,800. NHIS drives in Savelugu and Kumbungu districts assisted 350 households against a target of 6,000, down from 600, for a total of 3,680. The project equipped 2 facilities with delivery kits and solar vaccine fridges, bringing the total to 16 of 24, and conducted 6 supportive supervision visits against a target of 120. It trained 10 facility managers (6 female, 4 male) in respectful maternity care at Kumbungu District Health Directorate, for a life-of-Action total of 30 of 40. Verified DHIMS2/GHS records show ANC4+ at 59% (target 70), skilled birth attendance at 66% (target 75), Penta3 at 78% (target 88), postnatal checks within 48 hours at 46% (target 60), no tracer-medicine stock-out at 70% of facilities (target 85) and complete and timely DHIMS2 reporting at 86% (target 95). Project mobile teams kept emergency obstetric referrals running during the action, and clinics were rescheduled in June. Evidence comprises the verified outreach register, DHIMS2 extract, attendance sheet, NHIS register, handover document and supervision report. Priorities: hold the rescheduled June clinics, resume training and outreach, and install the remaining kits and fridges.

Penta3 coverage was the strongest outcome measure this month at 78% against an 88% target, and complete and timely DHIMS2 reporting stood at 86% against a 95% target. Several indicators fell behind plan because an industrial action by nurses and community health officers lasted about two weeks and closed or reduced services at many facilities in the five districts: outreach clinics fell to 18 against about 30 planned (5% of the 360 target), community education reached 900 women against a target of 18,000 (5% of target), ANC4+ dipped to 59% against a 70% target, and postnatal checks within 48 hours fell to 46% against a 60% target. The recorded corrective action was that training and outreach were suspended during the action, project mobile teams supported emergency services and kept emergency obstetric referrals running, and clinics were rescheduled in June. The indicators MR-OP1.1 (health workers trained in EmONC or IMNCI), MR-OP1.3 (CHVs trained and active) and MR-OP2.1 (DHMT staff trained in data use) were not reported this month; the officer's narrative records there was no training or CHV cohort in May. 3 Activities Implemented

The Action carried out six activities in May 2026, all dated within the reporting period, across Kumbungu and Savelugu districts. An industrial action by nurses and community health officers lasted about two weeks and closed or reduced services at many facilities in the five districts; training and outreach were suspended during the action and clinics were rescheduled in June.

On 12 May 2026 at the Kumbungu District Health Directorate, Kumbungu District, the project trained 10 facility managers in respectful maternity care (6 female, 4 male), bringing the life-of-Action total to 30 of 40. Verified evidence comprises the training attendance and session records. The records note that managers who hear women's exit-interview feedback commit to concrete changes.

On 14 May 2026 at Diare CHPS compound, Kumbungu District, the project delivered and installed delivery kits (delivery sets, neonatal resuscitation equipment and fetal dopplers) and solar vaccine fridges at 2 facilities, bringing the life-of-Action total to 16 of 24. Handover certificates were signed by the in-charges and logged by the district health directorate. Verified evidence is the signed handover approval document. The records note that technician handover and a daily temperature log at installation keep fridges working after the project.

On 17 May 2026 in Kumbungu District communities, CHVs and community mobilisers held education sessions on antenatal care, facility delivery and newborn danger signs, reaching 900 women (all recorded as female) against a target of 18,000; the life-of-Action total is 10,800 of 18,000. Verified evidence is the attendance sheet. The records note that market-day and water-point sessions reach more women than fixed venues.

On 19 May 2026 in Savelugu and Kumbungu districts, NHIA district officers and CHVs ran NHIS enrolment and renewal drives, registering or renewing 350 households against a target of 6,000; the life-of-Action total is 3,680 of 6,000. Verified evidence is the attached drive register. The records note that drives at antenatal clinics reach pregnant women directly.

On 20 May 2026 in the Savelugu Municipal Hospital catchment, Savelugu District, the project held 18 integrated outreach clinics offering ANC, immunisation, postnatal checks and child weighing, against a target of 360; the life-of-Action total is 219 of 360. Verified evidence comprises the outreach register and the DHIMS2 extract.

On 24 May 2026 in Savelugu District, district teams and project staff made 6 supportive supervision visits using the standard checklist covering partograph use, stock management, cold chain and DHIMS2 completeness, against a target of 120; the life-of-Action total is 70 of 120. Verified evidence is the attached supervision report.

Outreach clinics fell to 18 against about 30 planned for the month, and no EmONC/IMNCI or community health volunteer training cohort took place in May, because of the two-week industrial action. The officer's narrative records that project mobile teams kept emergency obstetric referrals running during the action and supported emergency services.

Five of the six outcome indicators recorded in May 2026 fell on the previous period, and one rose. Complete and timely DHIMS2 reporting stood at 86 percent against a target of 95, down from 89. Facilities with no stock-out of tracer MNCH medicines were at 70 percent against a target of 85, down from 72. ANC4+ was 59 percent against a target of 70, down from 61; skilled deliveries were 66 percent against a target of 75, down from 67; and postnatal checks within 48 hours were 46 percent against a target of 60, down from 48. Penta3 coverage rose to 78 percent against a target of 88, up from 76. The indicator comment attributes the dip in postnatal checks to the industrial action, which the section Activities Implemented describes; all findings carry a neutral evaluation, so the report records no performance judgement.

The Action reports outcome indicators from DHIMS2/GHS records as the donor instructions require. MR-OC1a to MR-OC1d and MR-OC2a draw on DHIMS2 / GHS records and MR-OC1e on facility stock cards. Commentary on data completeness rests on the recorded DHIMS2 reporting rate of 86 percent of facilities submitting complete and timely reports against a target of 95.

5 Challenges, Risks and Mitigation

The principal challenge of May 2026 was an industrial action by nurses and community health officers. It lasted about two weeks and closed or reduced services at many facilities in the five districts. Verified evidence attached to the month's activities records it: the outreach clinic field visit register and the DHIMS2 extract for the outreach activity, the supervision monitoring report, the training attendance sheet, the equipment handover document and the NHIS register all document the disruption and the reduced service levels it caused. As detailed in the Summary of the Month and Activities Implemented, the Action held 18 outreach clinics against a target of 360, reached 900 women against a target of 18,000, and assisted 350 households against a target of 6,000. The indicator updates record that clinics were suspended during the action and that postnatal checks dipped.

The project mitigated the disruption in three ways. Project mobile teams supported emergency services, and the officer's context records that these teams kept emergency obstetric referrals running during the industrial action. The mobile teams also held 18 clinics during the month, and the Action rescheduled remaining clinics in June. Delivery continued where facilities stayed open: the project installed delivery kits and solar vaccine fridges at 2 facilities on 14 May 2026 and trained 10 facility managers (6 female, 4 male) on 12 May 2026.

The inputs record no other challenge or risk for May 2026. No request for support or a change that needs the Contracting Authority's agreement was recorded for the month.

6 Cross-Cutting Issues: Gender, Environment and Do-No-Harm

An industrial action by nurses and community health officers that lasted about two weeks and closed or reduced services at many facilities shaped cross-cutting work in May 2026.

Gender. Women were prioritised in service delivery this month: the reporting officer's statement records that emergency obstetric care was prioritised during the industrial action and that project mobile teams ran antenatal clinics for women in Karaga. Sex-disaggregated results for the month show 10 facility managers trained in respectful maternity care (6 female, 4 male), per the verified attendance sheet, and community education sessions reached 900 women, all female as recorded. The outreach clinics, NHIS enrolment drives, equipment installation and supportive supervision visits did not record sex-disaggregated participant data in the records supplied for this section.

Environment. The officer's statement records that health-care waste management checks were paused for two weeks during the industrial action. No further environmental monitoring record was supplied for May 2026.

Do-no-harm. The statement records that the project did not replace striking staff and referred women to the nearest functioning facility. Beyond the industrial action itself, no incident or safeguarding concern was recorded in the records supplied for the month. No complaints, feedback or safeguarding compliance documentation beyond this statement was supplied for the period.

7 Visibility and Communication

No dedicated communication activity took place in May 2026. The Action's visible community-facing events this month were the 10-participant respectful maternity care training for facility managers at Kumbungu District Health Directorate, the delivery kits and solar vaccine fridges installed at 2 facilities in Kumbungu District, the community education sessions that reached 900 women, the 18 integrated outreach clinics, the 350-household NHIS drives and the 6 supportive supervision visits, as described in the section Activities Implemented.

Compliance status is partial. Verified evidence records these events: the verified training records held with the respectful maternity care activity, the verified handover documentation for the delivered kits and fridges, the verified community session attendance record, the verified outreach register and DHIMS2 extract, the verified NHIS drive register and the verified supervision report. The inputs do not include a dedicated record confirming EU visibility on the delivered materials, equipment and events, so confirmation remains outstanding. No remediation steps are recorded in the inputs; the recorded next steps are to install the remaining kits and fridges as stock arrives and to support managers in implementing their agreed action plans.

8 Plan for the Next Month

The Action will carry forward the plans recorded in May's activity records, after an industrial action by nurses and community health officers suspended training and outreach for about two weeks. The project will hold the integrated outreach clinics rescheduled into June, and review outreach routes against the road conditions, after 18 clinics were held in May (219 of 360 to date). Project mobile teams, which kept emergency obstetric referrals running during the action, will continue supporting emergency services. The remaining delivery kits and solar vaccine fridges will be installed as stock arrives, following 2 facilities equipped in May (16 of 24 to date), with technicians continuing the handover practice of a daily temperature log. The project will support the 10 facility managers trained in respectful maternity care in May (6 female, 4 male; 30 of 40 to date) to put their agreed action plans in place. Community education sessions on antenatal care, facility delivery and newborn danger signs will continue, with radio spots added, after sessions reached 900 women in May (10800 of 18,000 to date); the records note that sessions at market days and water points reach more women than sessions at fixed venues. NHIS enrolment and renewal drives will continue, with follow-up on renewals due next month, after 350 households were assisted in May (3680 of 6,000 to date). District teams will follow up the actions agreed at each facility during supportive supervision, after 6 visits in May (70 of 120 to date). No numerical targets or expected results for June 2026 were recorded in the inputs, so none are stated here.
