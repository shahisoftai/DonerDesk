[DEMO-7] NORTHERN GHANA MATERNAL, NEWBORN & CHILD HEALTH RESILIENCE PROGRAMME (MNCH-R) · SEP 1, 2026 – SEP 30, 2026 · DUE OCT 5, 2026

[DEMO-7] Northern Ghana Maternal, Newborn & Child Health Resilience Programme (MNCH-R) — final report

Approved
1 Executive Summary

The Action improved the use and quality of maternal, newborn and child health services across its twelve months, closing with most output targets met and outcome indicators close to their targets. The Northern Ghana Health Alliance (NGHA), with partner Tamale Community Health Network, implemented the twelve-month European Union-funded Action in 40 health facilities, including 24 CHPS compounds, in the Tolon, Kumbungu, Savelugu, Nanton and Karaga districts of Ghana's Northern Region. The Action handed over to the five district health management teams after completing the end-line survey and final district scorecards.

Delivery reached target in six output areas: 240 health workers trained in EmONC or IMNCI against a target of 240 (168 female and 72 male over the life of the Action), 300 community health volunteers (180 female and 120 male), 60 DHMT staff trained in data use (44 female and 16 male), 40 facility managers trained in respectful maternity care (24 female and 16 male), 20 quarterly district scorecards against a target of 20, and 6,000 households assisted with NHIS enrolment or renewal against a target of 6,000. Four outputs finished slightly below target: 23 of 24 facilities equipped (95.8% of target), 334 of 360 outreach clinics held (92.8%), 17,000 of 18,000 women reached by community education (94.4%) and 112 of 120 supportive supervision visits (93.3%). At outcome level, complete and timely DHIMS2 reporting reached 95 percent against a target of 95, from a baseline of 71. The institutional maternal mortality ratio fell from a baseline of 168 to 131 per 100,000 live births, against a target of 120; the officer's close-out records the target was missed although the ratio fell.

Recorded disruptions shaped delivery: an industrial action in May 2026 closed or reduced services for about two weeks, and flooding in July and August 2026 cut road access in Savelugu and Nanton; the Action re-planned outreach on higher ground routes and re-sequenced equipment deliveries to accessible districts first. Handover meetings were held in each district before staff contracts ended.

2 Context and Description of the Action

Maternal, newborn and child health outcomes in Ghana's Northern Region faced persistent gaps when the Action began. Baselines recorded 52 percent of pregnant women completing four or more antenatal care visits, 58 percent of deliveries attended by a skilled birth attendant, and an institutional maternal mortality ratio of 168 per 100,000 live births. The Action was funded through grant EU-GH/2025/HD/0417 under Global Gateway human development. It ran for twelve months and was implemented by the Northern Ghana Health Alliance (NGHA) with partner Tamale Community Health Network (TCHN) in the Tolon, Kumbungu, Savelugu, Nanton and Karaga districts.

The Action aimed to improve the use and quality of maternal, newborn and child health services in 40 health facilities, including 24 CHPS compounds. Its design combined five elements. It trained and mentored health workers in EmONC and IMNCI. It equipped facilities with delivery kits and solar vaccine fridges. It trained community health volunteers and ran integrated outreach clinics and community education. It also strengthened District Health Management Teams in data use, supportive supervision, quarterly scorecards, NHIS enrolment and respectful maternity care. Results were disaggregated by sex. EU co-financing was 90 percent and the implementer contributed 10 percent.

Delivery over the life of the Action is documented in verified records. Training registers, procurement and handover records, and outreach and community session registers evidence the output flows. Monitoring reports verified each month record the supportive supervision visits. The Action trained 240 health workers in EmONC or IMNCI against a target of 240 (168 female and 72 male, life-of-Action figures). It equipped 23 of 24 facilities and trained 300 CHVs against a target of 300 (180 female and 120 male). It held 334 outreach clinics against a target of 360 and reached 17,000 women through community education against a target of 18,000.

These records show steady progression of training cohorts and equipment installation through the twelve months. Close-out activities in September 2026 included an end-line survey, final scorecards and handover to the five DHMTs. Taken together, the design and delivery records indicate that the Action pursued its intended results through the mechanisms set out in its description. Outcomes moved as intended: skilled birth attendance rose to 72 percent against a baseline of 58 percent and a target of 75 percent.

2.1 Context Update

The Action worked in five districts of Ghana's Northern Region — Tolon, Kumbungu, Savelugu, Nanton and Karaga — with 40 health facilities, including 24 CHPS compounds, and with the Tamale Community Health Network, five District Health Management Teams and community health volunteers. Over the twelve months of implementation the health context in these districts was disrupted on three recorded occasions. In May 2026, an industrial action by nurses and community health officers lasted about two weeks and closed or reduced services at many facilities in the five districts; training and outreach were suspended during the action, and project mobile teams supported emergency services while clinics were rescheduled in June. In July and August 2026, flooding and the lean season cut road access in Savelugu and Nanton for several days and two CHPS compounds closed temporarily; outreach in the flood-affected communities was re-planned on higher ground routes and community health volunteers delivered antenatal care reminders on foot. In August 2026, an equipment shipment was held at customs, delaying the last delivery kits. Against this backdrop, service indicators at supported facilities ended the Action above their baselines: 66 percent of pregnant women completed four or more antenatal care visits against a baseline of 52 percent, 72 percent of deliveries were attended by a skilled birth attendant against a baseline of 58 percent, and 95 percent of facilities submitted complete and timely DHIMS2 reports against a baseline of 71 percent. The institutional maternal mortality ratio at supported facilities stood at 131 per 100,000 live births against a baseline of 168, though it missed the target of 120.

2.2 Intervention Logic

The Action assumed that trained and equipped facilities (Output 1), combined with stronger district health management and community demand (Output 2), would raise the use and quality of maternal, newborn and child health services, improving outcome indicators and ultimately reducing maternal mortality. Over the twelve months this logic largely held.

At output level, delivery was strong. The Action trained 240 health workers in EmONC or IMNCI against a target of 240 (100% of target), of whom 168 female and 72 male over the life of the Action. It equipped 23 of 24 facilities with delivery kits and solar vaccine fridges (95.8% of target), trained 300 community health volunteers against a target of 300 (180 female, 120 male over the life of the Action), held 334 outreach clinics against a target of 360 (92.8%), reached 17,000 women through community education against a target of 18,000 (94.4%), trained 60 DHMT staff in data use (44 female, 16 male), conducted 112 supportive supervision visits against a target of 120 (93.3%), produced 20 quarterly district scorecards against a target of 20 (100%), assisted 6,000 households with NHIS enrolment or renewal against a target of 6,000 (100%), and trained 40 facility managers in respectful maternity care (24 female, 16 male).

These outputs moved the outcome indicators close to their targets. Skilled birth attendance rose from a baseline of 58% to 72% against a target of 75%; four or more ANC visits rose from 52% to 66% against a target of 70%; Penta3 coverage rose from 71% to 86% against a target of 88%; postnatal checks within 48 hours rose from 34% to 57% against a target of 60%; and facilities with no tracer-medicine stock-out rose from 55% to 83% against a target of 85%. Governance outcomes also improved: complete and timely DHIMS2 reporting reached 95% against a target of 95%, active NHIS membership rose from 48% to 63% against a target of 65%, and women's satisfaction with respectful maternity care rose from 62% to 78% against a target of 80%.

The causal chain from outputs to outcomes therefore held, with small shortfalls concentrated in a few delivery targets (facilities equipped, outreach clinics, women reached, supervision visits). At impact level the logic held only in part: the institutional maternal mortality ratio fell from a baseline of 168 to 131 per 100,000 live births, but missed the target of 120. Supportive supervision records from the final period, verified as monitoring reports, document the checklist-based visits that underpinned the district-level outcome gains.

3 Assessment of Implementation

The Action closed after twelve months, and this section judges implementation against the Logical Framework Matrix using the verified life-of-Action figures, keeping outputs apart from outcomes.

Output delivery reached or effectively reached target in most areas. The Action trained 240 health workers in EmONC or IMNCI, 100% of the target of 240; the life-of-Action total comprised 168 female and 72 male. It trained 300 community health volunteers (100% of target; 180 female and 120 male), 60 DHMT staff in data use (100% of target; 44 female and 16 male) and 40 facility managers in respectful maternity care (100% of target; 24 female and 16 male). The Action assisted 6,000 households with NHIS enrolment or renewal (100% of the target of 6,000) and produced 20 quarterly district scorecards (100% of the target of 20). Delivery fell slightly short on three output indicators: the Action equipped 23 facilities with delivery kits and solar vaccine fridges against a target of 24 (95.8% of target), held 334 outreach clinics against 360 (92.8% of target) and conducted 112 supportive supervision visits against 120 (93.3% of target). Community education reached 17,000 women against a target of 18,000 (94.4% of target).

Outcome and impact indicators moved toward their targets without meeting them. The institutional maternal mortality ratio stood at 131 per 100,000 live births, against a baseline of 168 and a target of 120. ANC4+ coverage reached 66 percent against a baseline of 52 and a target of 70; skilled birth attendance reached 72 percent against a baseline of 58 and a target of 75; Penta3 coverage reached 86 percent against a baseline of 71 and a target of 88; postnatal checks within 48 hours reached 57 percent against a baseline of 34 and a target of 60; and stock-out-free facilities reached 83 percent against a baseline of 55 and a target of 85. DHIMS2 reporting reached 95 percent, meeting the target exactly. NHIS coverage reached 63 percent against a baseline of 48 and a target of 65, and satisfaction with respectful maternity care reached 78 percent against a baseline of 62 and a target of 80. The recorded explanation for the missed impact target is that the maternal mortality ratio of 131 against a target of 120 was missed although the ratio fell from 168; the officer's narrative attributes the near-miss output shortfalls to close-out workload that coincided with the end of staff contracts.

Read together, the Logframe shows delivery of every output at or close to target and steady movement on every outcome indicator from its baseline, with most outcome targets narrowly unmet. The evidence base comprises verified training registers, procurement and handover records, outreach and session registers, supervision reports, DHMT review minutes and NHIA drive registers.

3.3 Activities

Over the twelve months of the Action, delivery focused on two output areas: training and equipping facilities and communities (Output 1), and strengthening district health management and demand for services (Output 2). This section summarises the activities implemented under each Outcome; the verified figures themselves are reported in "Output Delivery" and "Impact and Outcome Results".

Under Output 1, the Action trained 240 health workers in EmONC or IMNCI with on-site mentoring against a target of 240 (100% of target; 168 female and 72 male over the life of the Action). Training records from within the reporting period show 20 participants (14 female, 6 male) attending the September 2026 cohort at Kumbungu Health Centre in Kumbungu District, which completed the target. The Action installed delivery kits — delivery sets, neonatal resuscitation equipment and fetal dopplers — and solar vaccine fridges at 23 facilities against a target of 24 (96% of target); the final September 2026 installation covered 2 facilities at Diare CHPS compound in Kumbungu District, with handover certificates signed by the in-charges and logged by the district health directorate. The Action trained 300 community health volunteers (100% of target; 180 female and 120 male over the life of the Action), noting that this finding is marked as based on partial records and that disaggregated data was not recorded for the current period. It held 334 integrated outreach clinics against a target of 360 (93% of target), including 34 clinics in September 2026 in the Savelugu Municipal Hospital catchment covering antenatal care, immunisation, postnatal checks and child weighing. Community education sessions on antenatal care, facility delivery and newborn danger signs reached 17,000 women against a target of 18,000 (94% of target), with 1,850 women reached in September 2026 in Kumbungu District communities.

Under Output 2, the Action trained 60 DHMT staff in DHIMS2 and scorecard data use (100% of target; 44 female and 16 male over the life of the Action), delivered 112 supportive supervision visits using a standard checklist against a target of 120 (93% of target, including 12 visits in September 2026 in the Savelugu District), produced 20 quarterly district scorecards against a target of 20 (100% of target), assisted 6,000 households with NHIS enrolment or renewal against a target of 6,000 (100% of target, including 760 households in September 2026 drives in Savelugu and Tolon Districts), and trained 40 facility managers in respectful maternity care (100% of target; 24 female and 16 male over the life of the Action).

Taken together, the activity records show that all training targets were met, while equipment installation, outreach clinics, community education and supervision visits finished slightly below target. The officer's close-out context records that the end-line survey, final scorecards and handover to the five District Health Management Teams were completed, and that handover meetings were held in each district before staff contracts ended.

3.4 Deviations from Plan and Variance

Several outputs and the impact indicator finished below their life-of-Action targets, while most outputs reached target. The institutional maternal mortality ratio recorded 131 per 100,000 live births against a target of 120, having fallen from a baseline of 168. Four output results closed slightly below target: the Action equipped 23 of 24 facilities with delivery kits and solar vaccine fridges (96% of target), held 334 of 360 outreach clinics (92.8% of target), reached 17,000 of 18,000 women through community education sessions (94% of target), and conducted 112 of 120 supportive supervision visits (93% of target).

What caused each deviation and what was done about it? The verified activity records attribute the shortfalls to specific recorded events. Poor access roads to two CHPS compounds required more solar vaccine fridge installation days than planned; installation teams worked in pairs and technicians trained facility staff on daily temperature logging at handover, but the final delivery kits were further delayed in August 2026 when flooding and an equipment shipment held at customs pushed deliveries back; the Action re-sequenced deliveries to the accessible districts first and followed up clearance weekly with the clearing agent. An industrial action by nurses and community health officers in May 2026 closed or reduced services for about two weeks and suspended training and outreach; project mobile teams supported emergency services and clinics were rescheduled in June. Flooding and the lean season cut road access in Savelugu and Nanton in July and August 2026; outreach was re-planned on higher ground routes and community health volunteers delivered antenatal care reminders on foot. Close-out workload (the end-line survey, final scorecards and handover) coincided with the end of staff contracts; handover meetings were held in each district before staff contracts ended. Officer narrative context explains that these recorded disruptions left the four output results and the impact target slightly below plan.

Outputs that reached or exceeded plan are reported in Output Delivery: 240 health workers trained in EmONC or IMNCI, 300 community health volunteers, 60 DHMT staff, 40 facility managers, 20 district scorecards and 6,000 households assisted with NHIS enrolment or renewal, all at 100% of target. Outcome indicators also moved towards their targets without reaching them; those results are assessed in Impact and Outcome Results and are not repeated here.

4 Assessment Against Evaluation Criteria

This section assesses the Action against the OECD-DAC evaluation criteria — relevance, effectiveness, efficiency, impact and sustainability — using the verified life-of-Action figures; the criteria are then discussed in dedicated sections elsewhere in this report (Relevance, Effectiveness, Efficiency, Sustainability, Impact), which this assessment summarises without restating their full detail.

Relevance: the Action addressed recorded gaps in maternal, newborn and child health services in five districts of the Northern Region, aligning with district health priorities and the Global Gateway human development focus of grant EU-GH/2025/HD/0417. No additional relevance evidence beyond the project description and baselines was recorded.

Effectiveness: the Action met or closely approached most targets. It trained 240 health workers in EmONC or IMNCI (100% of the target of 240; life-of-Action breakdown 168 female and 72 male), 300 community health volunteers (100% of the target of 300; 180 female and 120 male; disaggregated data was not recorded for the final period), 60 DHMT staff in data use (100% of the target of 60; 44 female and 16 male; based on partial records, as data were last reported as of 2026-07-31), and 40 facility managers in respectful maternity care (100% of the target of 40; 24 female and 16 male; disaggregated data was not recorded for the final period, records as of 2026-07-31). It produced 20 quarterly district scorecards (100% of target), assisted 6,000 households with NHIS enrolment or renewal (100% of target) and equipped 23 of 24 facilities (96% of target). Outcome indicators approached but mostly did not reach their targets: 66 percent ANC4+ (target 70), 72 percent skilled delivery (target 75), 86 percent Penta3 (target 88), 57 percent postnatal checks within 48 hours (target 60), 83 percent facilities without tracer-medicine stock-outs (target 85), 95 percent complete and timely DHIMS2 reporting (target 95), 63 percent NHIS coverage (target 65) and 78 percent satisfaction with respectful maternity care (target 80). Outreach reached 334 of 360 clinics (93% of target) and 17,000 of 18,000 women (94% of target); supervision conducted 112 of 120 visits (93% of target).

Efficiency: against a budget of 1,800,000 EUR the Action expended 1,712,400 EUR, a burn rate of 95.1 percent; the Financial Summary sets out the line-level detail.

Impact: the institutional maternal mortality ratio fell to 131 per 100,000 live births from a baseline of 168, against a target of 120 (109% of the target level, expressed as a ratio rather than an achievement); the indicator comment records that the target of 120 was not met.

Sustainability: all five districts adopted the scorecard in their quarterly review agendas, and handover meetings were held in each district before staff contracts ended; the Sustainability and Exit Strategy section elaborates.

In sum, the Action delivered its output targets in full or near-full across both outcome areas, brought most outcome indicators close to their targets, and improved the impact indicator while missing its target of 120.

4.1 Relevance

The Action responded directly to the recorded needs of women and children in the five target districts of Ghana's Northern Region and aligned with national health policy. At the start of the Action, the baseline gaps in maternal, newborn and child health services were substantial: 52 percent of pregnant women completed four or more antenatal care visits, 58 percent of deliveries were attended by a skilled birth attendant, and the institutional maternal mortality ratio stood at 168 per 100,000 live births. These gaps defined the problems the Action set out to address for women and children in Tolon, Kumbungu, Savelugu, Nanton and Karaga districts.

The evidence of the Action's relevance lies in the match between its outputs and these needs. Over the life of the Action, the Action trained 240 health workers in EmONC or IMNCI against a target of 240 (100% of target; 168 female and 72 male), trained and activated 300 community health volunteers (100% of target; 180 female and 120 male), and equipped 23 of 24 facilities with delivery kits and solar vaccine fridges (95.8% of target). These interventions responded to the need for skilled attendance and functional service delivery points, which the baseline figures showed were lacking.

The Action also addressed the wider policy context. It strengthened District Health Management Teams in data use, supportive supervision, quarterly scorecards, NHIS enrolment and respectful maternity care, in line with the Government of Ghana's health sector priorities of improving data quality, financial protection through NHIS, and the quality of maternity care. By the close of the Action, 95 percent of facilities submitted complete and timely DHIMS2 reports, 6000 households had been assisted with NHIS enrolment or renewal (100% of the target of 6000), and 78 percent of women interviewed were satisfied with respectful maternity care against a baseline of 62 percent. All five districts adopted the scorecard in their quarterly review agendas, embedding the tools in routine district practice.

The Action was therefore relevant to the needs of women and children and to national policy. Its focus on raising the use and quality of maternal, newborn and child health services addressed the baseline gaps in antenatal care attendance, skilled delivery, postnatal checks, immunisation and medicine availability, while its emphasis on district health management, NHIS enrolment and respectful maternity care corresponded to established national policy commitments in the health sector.

4.2 Effectiveness

The expected outcomes were substantially achieved: two of the nine outcome indicators reached or met their targets, and the remainder closed close to target, while the impact indicator improved on its baseline but missed its target.

The Action met its DHIMS2 reporting outcome in full: 95 percent of facilities submitted complete and timely reports against a target of 95 percent, up from a baseline of 71 percent. Every other outcome indicator recorded gains on baseline and finished between one and five points below target. Four or more ANC visits reached 66 percent against a target of 70 percent (baseline 52 percent); skilled birth attendance reached 72 percent against a target of 75 percent (baseline 58 percent); Penta3 coverage among children under 1 year reached 86 percent against a target of 88 percent (baseline 71 percent); postnatal checks within 48 hours reached 57 percent against a target of 60 percent (baseline 34 percent); facilities with no stock-out of tracer MNCH medicines reached 83 percent against a target of 85 percent (baseline 55 percent); active NHIS membership among target households reached 63 percent against a target of 65 percent (baseline 48 percent); and women's satisfaction with respectful maternity care reached 78 percent against a target of 80 percent (baseline 62 percent).

At impact level, the institutional maternal mortality ratio at supported facilities recorded 131 per 100,000 live births against a target of 120, having fallen from a baseline of 168; the indicator update notes that the target was not met. Outcome and output results are kept apart here: output delivery, reported in "Output Delivery", largely reached target and underpinned these outcome gains. The officer's close-out narrative records that outreach clinics finished at 334 of 360, women reached at 17,000 of 18,000, supervision visits at 112 of 120 and equipped facilities at 23 of 24, each slightly below target. This section draws on the verified DHIMS2 and GHS records, the facility stock cards, the quarterly household spot survey, the quarterly exit interviews and the maternal death audit as its data sources; the underlying figures are presented in "Impact and Outcome Results".

The Action delivered most results on time and at reasonable cost. It spent 1712400 EUR of the 1800000 EUR budget, a burn rate of 95.1 percent, leaving a balance of 87600 EUR. Delivery matched this spending closely: 240 of 240 health workers trained in EmONC or IMNCI (100% of target), 300 of 300 community health volunteers trained and active (100% of target), 6000 of 6000 households assisted with NHIS enrolment or renewal (100% of target) and 20 of 20 district scorecards produced (100% of target). Four outputs closed just below target: 23 of 24 facilities equipped (95.8% of target), 334 of 360 outreach clinics (92.8% of target), 17000 of 18000 women reached (94% of target) and 112 of 120 supervision visits (93.3% of target).

Travel and supportive supervision reached 99.3 percent, reflecting the 112 completed visits. Monitoring, evaluation, visibility and audit stood at 61.5 percent, with a balance of 23100 EUR, the largest unspent line.

On timeliness, the Action completed its outputs within the twelve-month duration despite three recorded disruptions (see Context Update). Close-out work — the end-line survey, final scorecards and handover to the five DHMTs — was completed before the end of staff contracts, and handover meetings were held in each district. Evidence for supervision delivery comes from verified monitoring reports. Overall, results were delivered at reasonable cost and broadly on time; the shortfall in outreach, community education, supervision and equipping was small and the officer's close-out narrative records that these finished slightly below target.

4.4 Sustainability

The results most likely to continue after the Action ends are the district scorecard system, the trained workforce, the equipped facilities and the NHIS enrolment function. The five District Health Management Teams adopted the quarterly scorecard in their budget and review agendas; over the life of the Action the districts produced 20 scorecards against a target of 20. Trained health workers, community health volunteers and DHMT staff remain in post with the Ghana Health Service, so the capacity built through training (240 health workers in EmONC or IMNCI, 300 community health volunteers, 60 DHMT staff and 40 facility managers, as reported in 'Output Delivery') stays within the district health system. NHIS enrolment and renewal drives continue under the district NHIA offices, building on the 6000 households assisted against a target of 6000. The handover to the five DHMTs was completed before staff contracts ended, which supports the continuation of these functions within the districts.

5 Cross-Cutting Issues

The Action operated in a context where women carry most of the maternal and newborn health burden, so gender equality ran through its design. The mandatory question on sex-disaggregated results is answered as follows: the training outputs recorded sex-disaggregated life-of-Action breakdowns, which "Assessment of Implementation" reports in full; women formed the majority in each trained group, matching the female-dominated maternal health workforce and client base, and the inputs record no identified gender gap in access to the trainings. The Action did not record a dedicated gender analysis, gender-focused activity, or any climate and environment indicator, so this report cannot assess those areas beyond the facts above. On the rights-based approach, the Action trained 40 facility managers in respectful maternity care (24 female and 16 male), reaching 100% of the target, and satisfaction among women with respectful maternity care closed at 78 percent against a baseline of 62 and a target of 80. Monthly monitoring reports recorded challenges relating to service disruptions, road access, close-out workload and equipment logistics rather than gender, climate or rights-based programming; the Action recorded no mitigation steps specific to gender equality, climate and environment, or the rights-based approach.

6 Risks and Assumptions

Over the twelve months of the Action, the core assumptions of the Logical Framework Matrix held: district health management teams remained committed, vaccines and trained staff were available, and roads remained passable in the dry season. Several recorded risks nevertheless materialised. In February 2026 a national Penta vaccine shortage occurred; in May 2026 an industrial action by nurses and community health officers lasted about two weeks; in July and August 2026 flooding and the lean season cut road access in Savelugu and Nanton; and in August 2026 an equipment shipment was delayed at customs, holding up the last delivery kits. Supervision monitoring records document these disruptions and the facility-level follow-up during the affected months. The Action mitigated each risk through recorded steps: recall lists addressed the vaccine shortage, mobile teams sustained emergency services during the industrial action, outreach routes were re-planned on higher ground and community health volunteers delivered antenatal care reminders on foot during the flooding, and the shipment clearance was followed up weekly with the clearing agent. These measures limited the effect on delivery, which closed slightly below target only for a small number of outputs, as reported in 'Output Delivery' and 'Deviations from Plan and Variance'.

8 Lessons Learned

The Action recorded four main lessons for future programming, drawn from its twelve months of delivery.

First, learning sticks when supervision follows training quickly. The Action trained 240 health workers in EmONC or IMNCI against a target of 240 (100% of target), and training teams observed that mentoring at the facility within a month of the classroom course improves use of the partograph and neonatal resuscitation. Future programmes should budget mentoring visits as an integral part of every training cohort rather than as a follow-on option.

Second, local data and local venues drive participation. Data-use training on the district's own records proved more useful than generic examples, and scorecards presented with district budgets drew faster action from the district assemblies; all five districts adopted the scorecard in their quarterly review agendas. On the demand side, sessions at market days and water points reached more women than sessions at fixed venues, training community health volunteers at the nearest CHPS compound reduced absenteeism, fixed outreach calendars shared with CHVs and chiefs raised turnout, and NHIS drives at antenatal clinics reached pregnant women directly. Supportive supervision added a related finding: a checklist with agreed actions, reviewed at the next visit, closes gaps faster than reports alone.

Third, equipment handover determines whether hardware survives the project. Installation teams found that technician handover and a daily temperature log at installation keep solar vaccine fridges working after the project; poor access roads to two CHPS compounds required extra installation days, which contributed to 23 of 24 facilities equipped (95.8% of target).

Fourth, close-out needs protection. The end-line survey, final scorecards and handover to the five DHMTs coincided with the end of staff contracts, and handover meetings had to be held in each district before contracts ended. The Action also absorbed a two-week industrial action in May 2026, a June backlog of postponed clinics and trainings, flooding in Savelugu and Nanton in July and August, and an equipment shipment held at customs; outcomes still closed close to target, with ANC4+ at 66 percent, skilled delivery at 72 percent and the maternal mortality ratio at 131 per 100,000 live births against a baseline of 168.

What would you do differently? On the recorded evidence, three adjustments follow. Build a contingency buffer into procurement and installation schedules so that road access, customs clearance and equipment stock do not compress final deliveries. Ring-fence staffing time for close-out activities — end-line survey, final scorecards and handover — rather than allowing them to coincide with contract end dates. And embed the practices that worked from the outset: on-site mentoring within a month of training, scorecards presented alongside district budgets, and supervision checklists whose agreed actions are reviewed at the next visit.

9 Sustainability and Exit Strategy

The Action structured its exit around a complete transfer of assets, systems and responsibilities to the District Health Management Teams and the Ghana Health Service. Before staff contracts ended, the implementer held handover meetings in each of the five districts — Tolon, Kumbungu, Savelugu, Nanton and Karaga.

The evidence for the transfer is recorded in the close-out monitoring reports and the officer's exit statement. The Action transferred scorecard templates, supervision checklists, training materials and the equipment register to the District Directors of Health Services. The Regional Health Directorate received the final data-use dashboard. No assets remain with the implementer.

These transfers build on systems the districts already ran with Action support: all five districts adopted the quarterly scorecard in their review agendas, and DHMTs produced 20 quarterly district scorecards against a target of 20 (100% of target). The supportive supervision checklist used in 112 of 120 visits (93.3% of target) and the training materials behind the 240 health workers trained in EmONC or IMNCI (100% of target) now sit with the district authorities that will continue using them. Outcome results recorded at close-out — such as 95 percent of facilities submitting complete and timely DHIMS2 reports and 63 percent of target households covered by active NHIS membership — were produced through the district data and insurance systems the transferred dashboard, templates and registers support.

The handover sequencing mitigated the close-out challenge the officer recorded: the end-line survey, final scorecards and handover workload coincided with the end of staff contracts. Holding the meetings in each district before contracts ended meant the transfer completed while staff were still in post. As of the close of the Action, the recorded position is that all assets, systems and responsibilities transferred; no further arrangements are recorded beyond the district and regional adoptions noted above.

11 Recommendations

The Action closed with strong output delivery and outcome indicators close to target, but with several results slightly below plan. The Action trained 240 health workers in EmONC or IMNCI against a target of 240 (100% of target), produced 20 of 20 quarterly district scorecards (100% of target) and assisted 6000 of 6000 households with NHIS enrolment or renewal (100% of target). Four outputs finished slightly below target: 23 of 24 facilities equipped (95.8% of target), 334 of 360 outreach clinics held (92.8% of target), 17000 of 18000 women reached by community education (94% of target) and 112 of 120 supportive supervision visits conducted (93.3% of target). The institutional maternal mortality ratio reached 131 per 100,000 live births against a target of 120, having fallen from a baseline of 168.

These results carry two lessons recorded during implementation. First, close-out workload — the end-line survey, final scorecards and handover — coincided with the end of staff contracts, and handover meetings were held in each district before contracts ended. Second, mentoring at the facility within a month of the classroom course improved use of the partograph and neonatal resuscitation, and all five districts adopted the scorecard in their quarterly review agendas.

The Contracting Authority should allow implementing organisations to phase staff contracts so that close-out duties do not overlap with contract expiry, protecting the completion of final surveys and handover. It should also note that the monitoring, evaluation, visibility and audit budget line closed at a burn rate of 61.5 percent (expenditure of 36900 against a budget of 60000, balance 23100), which suggests scope to plan end-line measurement more realistically.

The Ghana Health Service and the five DHMTs should maintain the quarterly district scorecard in their review agendas, as all five districts have adopted it, and should continue the supervision checklist practice of agreeing actions and reviewing them at the next visit, which activity records show closes gaps faster than reports alone.

Implementing partners should schedule on-site mentoring within one month of each classroom course, following the recorded lesson on partograph and neonatal resuscitation use; build installation and delivery schedules that allow extra days for facilities reached by poor access roads, which delayed solar vaccine fridge installation; and equip facilities early in the implementation period, since procurement and customs delays contributed to 23 of 24 facilities being equipped by close-out.
