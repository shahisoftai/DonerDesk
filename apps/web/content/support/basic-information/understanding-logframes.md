# Understanding Logframes

A **results framework** says what your project aims to change and why each result leads to the next. A **logframe** (logical framework) sets the same results out as a measurement matrix: indicators, baselines, targets, means of verification and assumptions. DonorDesk keeps **one set of results** and lets you read it either way, so the two can never disagree. It organises your indicators, evidence and report sections.

## The four levels

| Level | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | What long-term change do we contribute to? | Reduced child malnutrition in District X. |
| **Outcome** | What change do we expect among beneficiaries? | Improved feeding practices. |
| **Output** | What do we deliver? | 400 caregivers trained. |
| **Activity** | What do we do? | Counselling sessions. |

Each item has a **code** (OUT-1), a title and a parent. Items form a tree you can rearrange by drag-and-drop. Each can also carry:

- **Why this leads to the result above it**: the reasoning (the results framework's causal statement).
- **Assumptions**: what must hold for this result to lead to the one above it (the logframe's assumptions column).

## Your donor's words

Donors name the levels differently: USAID speaks of a Goal, Intermediate Results and Sub-Intermediate Results; FCDO of Impact, Outcome and Output; the European Union of an Overall objective, Specific objectives and Results. Choose your donor's terms in **Settings → Setup → Reporting profile → Results framework wording** (or on the wizard's Start step). Only the words on screen and in downloaded files change; your results are the same.

## Two ways to read it

On the **Plan → Results framework** page, switch between **Edit**, **Results framework** (the chain of results with their reasoning) and **Logframe** (every level with indicators, baselines, targets, means of verification and assumptions). Both can be downloaded as Word or PDF.

## Indicators

Each item can have indicators: number, percentage, yes/no, text, ratio, currency or custom. Each has baseline, target, unit and frequency, and optionally data source and means of verification, disaggregation and a calculation rule. See [Indicators and targets](/support/getting-started/indicators-and-targets).

## Why it matters

- The project is not reporting-ready without indicators.
- Evidence and activities link to indicators, which is how reports show proof.
- Verified indicator values feed the report's tables, charts and comparisons.

## Getting one in

Import from Excel/CSV (download the template on the Logframe page; optional **Assumptions** and **Rationale** columns are read when present) or build it by hand. See [How to build a logframe](/support/how-to/build-logframe). Every change is kept as a version: see [How to see the history of your results framework](/support/how-to/see-framework-history).
