# How to Use the Visual Studio

**Dashboards and infographics** in the side menu opens the Visual Studio. It is one place for designs, charts, maps, templates, files and alerts. Older addresses for charts, maps, dashboards and infographics now open the matching page here.

## The tabs

- **Designs**: your dashboards and infographics. Open one to edit, present, publish or share it.
- **Charts**: pick a project and build a chart (over 50 kinds). Add it to a design when it looks right.
- **Maps**: pick a project to see results by place, pins and drawn areas. Record movements between places here for a flow map.
- **Templates**: starter designs, including one for each sector.
- **Assets**: the project's images and brand kit.
- **Alerts**: tell yourself when an indicator crosses a limit (see [set an alert](/support/how-to/use-the-visual-studio#alerts)).
- **TV**: shows your designs one after another on a screen.

## Narrow what you see

In the filter pane choose a **Level** or an **Indicator** to look closer, or click a chart label. Nothing is saved. Clear the choice to go back.

## Motion

Select a block and use **Motion**: None, Fade in, Rise in or Count up (for a figure). A bar race has a **Play** button that steps through the periods. Files, printouts and people who prefer reduced motion always get the final picture.

## Who else is here

When someone else has the same design open, their name appears at the top and the block they selected is marked. This is only shown while they are there; nothing is stored.

## Email me this design

Choose **Email me this** and pick weekly or monthly. The email carries the design's title and a link, never the figures. You stop it with **Stop emailing me**. You are only emailed while you can still see the design.

## Alerts

On the **Alerts** tab pick an indicator and a limit. You are told once when it crosses the limit. A rate is never judged as a percent of target, and a value marked not applicable counts as no figure.

## Things the charts will not do

Charts refuse to mislead and tell you why: a rate is never added up, two units never share one axis, and a missing value is a gap, never zero. Some kinds need particular data (a violin needs enough values; an alluvial or chord follows spent money). A population pyramid is not offered because breakdowns are recorded one dimension at a time.

## Maps with two indicators or movements

- A **bivariate map** needs exactly two indicators and places with pins.
- A **flow map** draws movements you record on the Maps tab (from, to, what moves, how much). Only movements with a pin at both ends are drawn, and two units are never mixed. Movements never appear through a share link.
