# How to Create a Project

A **project** is one funded grant or programme with one donor and one reporting cycle. Only **Admins** can create projects. Check that you have a free project slot on your plan ([Plans and limits](/support/account-billing/plans-and-limits)); archived projects do not count.

You create a project with the **project wizard**: ten short slides, one decision at a time. **Nothing is created until you press Start the project on the last slide**, so you can fill the slides in any order, leave, and come back. Your answers are kept as a draft.

## Before you start

You do not need documents. You can answer every slide yourself. If you have a proposal, logframe, work plan or budget, the wizard can read it and propose answers for you to confirm (see "Start from documents" below).

It helps to have to hand: the donor's name, the project dates, your results (goal, outcomes, outputs, activities) and indicators, the places you work, and the budget.

## Step 1: Open the wizard

1. Click **New project** on **Projects**, or **+ New project** in the top bar.
2. The wizard opens on slide 1. Across the top are ten numbered tabs. Each shows how many questions are **still to answer**. A tab is red and says "n to answer" while something is missing on that slide. Click any tab to jump to it, or use **Next →** and **← Back**.
3. The bar **Still to answer (n)** lists every missing answer. Click one to go to it.

Read the yellow notice at the top once. The wizard reads documents with an AI that can miss information or misread a figure, so **check each item against your documents. You are responsible for what you accept.**

## Step 2: Go through the ten slides

### 1. Start
Say what kind of project it is.
- **Donor** and **Country**: type and pick from the suggestions.
- **Main sector**: this decides the starting answers and the indicator library you are offered. You can add other sectors too.
- Optional: **Choose files** (up to five documents) and **Read my documents** (see below).
- **Use the starting answers for this sector** fills in the usual reporting frequency, review mode, entry window, common risks and a starting dashboard. It only fills what is still empty, and you can change every answer afterwards. Your organisation can write its own starting answers in **Settings → Starting answers**.

### 2. Project and team
- **Project name**, **Project code** (must be unique in your workspace; the wizard tells you if it is taken), **Start date**, **End date**, **Currency** (for example USD) and a **Short summary**.
- **Team**: type a **Name** and **Email**, choose a **Role**, then **Add to team**. For people who do not have an account yet, set **Invitation**. They are invited when you launch and are added to the project when they join. The slide asks for a project manager and a monitoring and evaluation officer.
- **Outside partner (sees this project only)**: add anyone from another organisation here, or tick that there are none. See [How to invite an outside partner](/support/how-to/invite-an-outside-partner).

### 3. Results framework
What the project will achieve, from goal down to activities.
- **Choose from the library** offers ready-made results for your sector.
- **Add a result**: choose its **Kind** (goal, outcome, output, activity), write **What will be achieved**, and pick what it **Belongs to** (the result above it).
- Every outcome needs an output and every output needs an activity before the project can start.

### 4. Indicators
How you will know each result is reached.
- **Choose from the library** (you tick the indicators you want; they are copied into your project and are yours to edit) or **Add an indicator**.
- For each one, say **What will be measured**, what it **Measures**, its target, who owns it and how it is read.
- Each indicator must sit under a result. If one is not placed, the slide shows **Choose which result … measures**.
- Library indicators are suggestions. They start as **not yet reviewed**. Check each one before you rely on it.

### 5. Places
Where the project works.
- Pick the **Country**, then **Add a place** (its **Name** and what it is **Inside**, for example a district inside a region).
- Or tick **Not used for this project**.

### 6. Work plan
When and where each activity happens.
- **Add an activity**: name it, say which result it is **Part of**, then give dates, a place, a lead and the indicators it feeds.
- A note shows if some work **feeds no indicator**. This is a prompt, not an error.

### 7. Data entry
How the numbers will be collected, checked and proven.
- **How each indicator is collected**: choose a way for each indicator, such as typing values in, a form, or an import.
- **Checking values** (**How values are checked**): choose the review mode. In **simple review** a value that has a figure and a file is handed in and verified for you by someone who may verify; in the **full workflow** a person hands values in and a reviewer verifies them.
- **When values can be entered**: **Opens (days before the period ends)** and **Closes (days after the period ends)**. After the window closes, people who cannot verify can no longer enter values.

### 8. Reporting
- **How often the project reports**: monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, annual, final or custom.
- **Each kind of report**: **Add a kind of report** with its layout and due date.
- **The reporting calendar** shows every report that will be created.
- **Remind people before reports are due**: on or off.

### 9. Budget and finance
- **Funding**: **Add funding** with **From** (the funder), **Amount** and **Currency** (three letters, for example USD). Amounts must be written plainly (`1250.50`, not `1,250`).
- **Budget**: **Add a budget line** with a name and an amount. **Does it add up?** compares the lines with the funding.
- **Reporting currency**: choose the currency the project reports in.
- Or tick **Not used for this project** if you keep finance elsewhere.

### 10. Review and launch
- **Risks and assumptions**: **Add a risk or assumption** (**What it is**, **What it is about**, **How serious**), or tick that there are none.
- **Checklist**: lists anything still missing.
- **Starting dashboard**: choose one, or none.
- **Invitations**: shows who will be invited.
- **What will be created**: the complete list of what the launch will make.
- **Sign off**: confirm you have checked everything. Any later change clears the sign-off, so sign off last.
- **Start the project**.

## Step 3: Start the project

Press **Start the project**. The wizard creates the project, its results and indicators, places, work plan, budget, team, forms and dashboard in order, and shows each step as it finishes. If something stops it, you can **resume**; nothing is created twice. When it finishes you land on the project, which is **Active**.

Things you do **not** set in the wizard: who verifies, who approves reports and finance, the evidence rules and the compliance checklist. Verification and approval follow people's roles, confidentiality is chosen per file, and the checklist is built from the donor template. Set the report template in the project's **Templates** tab ([upload a donor template](/support/how-to/upload-donor-template)).

## Start from documents (optional)

On slide 1 click **Choose files** (a proposal, logframe, work plan or budget, up to five), then **Read my documents**.
1. Files are kept in your organisation's Google Drive, so Drive must be connected ([Connect Google Drive](/support/how-to/connect-google-drive)). The AI features must be turned on for your organisation.
2. Each slide then shows what was found, with **Show in the document** beside each item. Accept, edit or reject each one. Nothing is created until you launch.
3. A section of your document that is blank stays blank. The wizard says so rather than inventing content. Items it could not place are listed as **left out**.
4. **Read this part again** re-reads one part of a document.

## Edit or end a project later

Open the project's **Settings** tab to edit details, dates and budget and to set the status. See [How to archive a project](/support/how-to/archive-a-project).

## Try it safely first

The Academy **demo project** is a complete sample project that you can explore, and it does not count toward your limits. See [Academy tour](/support/how-to/use-the-academy-tour).
