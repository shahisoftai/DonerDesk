# The Sector Library

The sector library is a ready-made list of results and indicators for the sectors humanitarian and development projects work in, so you do not have to write every indicator from scratch. **Choosing from it is always your decision: nothing is added until you press Add.**

## What is in it

- 30 sectors (water, sanitation and hygiene, health, nutrition, food security, shelter, protection, education, cash, agriculture, climate and more) and four themes that apply to any project (protection and accountability to affected people, monitoring and learning, finance and grants, safeguarding).
- About 280 indicators, each with its definition, kind of number, how it is usually collected, which way is better, how to break it down and where it comes from.
- For each sector: starting questions for your assessment, suggested results (goal, outcomes, outputs and activities), usual risks and a starting dashboard.

Every indicator is marked **Not yet reviewed** until a person in your organisation reviews it (see below). Some wording was written from published standards such as the Sphere Handbook and is a starting point: check each indicator against your donor's requirements before you rely on it.

## A standard is a reference, not your target

Some indicators show a **reference standard**, for example "at most 20 people per toilet". It is shown beside your target so you can compare. It never sets your target, and it never changes the colour of your target. When you enter a value, a short line says whether it meets the reference or is below it. In the entry **Sheet** a small **Ref** tag sits beside the target of such an indicator (point at it to read the reference); the line with the comparison is shown for the row you are on, and in the **Form** view under each indicator.

## In the project wizard

1. On the **Start** step choose the **main sector**, and tick any other sectors the project works in. The sector's assessment questions appear; your answers are saved with the project description.
2. **Use the starting answers for this sector** fills only what is still empty (how often you report, report layouts, how values are checked, usual risks, a starting dashboard).
3. On **Results framework**, **Choose from the library** → **Add the suggested results** adds the sector's goal, outcomes, outputs and activities. Each outcome and output comes with a **starting assumption** (for example, that water points keep working and the source is not depleted). You can edit or remove any of them.
4. On **Indicators**, **Choose from the library**, tick the indicators you want (use the filters for result level, kind, "recommended only" or "with a reference standard") and press **Add**. Each indicator arrives with no baseline and no target: you set both.

An indicator is placed under the result it measures when its wording fits that result. **If no result fits, it is not placed by guess**: the wizard tells you "Choose which result … measures" and you pick one in the **Measures** box.

## On an existing project

Open the project's **Logframe** page and choose **Choose from the library**. Pick a result in the box at the bottom to put the indicators there, or leave it on automatic: indicators whose wording fits no result are skipped with "Choose where it goes", and you can add them again with a result chosen. Indicators from the library show a **Sector library** label and, where there is one, the reference standard.

The library is updated from time to time. When an indicator you added has a newer version, the project shows what changed and lets you accept each change separately. Anything you do not tick stays as you set it.

## How often an indicator is collected

Besides **Weekly, Monthly, Quarterly, Semi-annual** and **Annual**, an indicator can be collected **when something happens**: **Per survey, Per assessment, Per analysis, Per campaign, Per distribution, Per season** or **At construction**. Such an indicator is not asked for in an ordinary month. It is expected where you record a value and in the project's closing period.

## Review and your own indicators

Under **Settings → Sector library** an administrator can see how many indicators have been reviewed, download a reviewer sheet, and upload it back with each reviewer's decisions. An organisation can also add its own indicators, reword one for its own use or hide one it never uses. The built-in library is never changed, and another organisation never sees yours.

## What the library does not do

It never sets a baseline or a target, never judges your results against a standard, and never changes an indicator you already have without asking. Its starting assumptions are general conditions, not checked against your context: confirm each one before it goes to a donor. The text of the library is in English.
